Chapter Thirty-One — After the Smoke

The city was waking up around them, pale and noisy and ordinary, a strange contrast to the wreckage strewn across the lot behind the compound. Sirens had faded into the distance. Ambulances rolled. Police tape went up. And in the wake of all the violence, the people who had made it through were trying very hard not to fall apart.

Rin sat on the curb with her knees drawn up, elbows on them, staring at the cracked pavement. Her shoulder ached. Her ribs hurt. Her whole body felt like it had been run through a grinder and assembled back wrong. She was covered in dirt and blood and the kind of exhaustion that made the world feel hazy around the edges. The adrenaline had gone, and what was left in its place was a thick, unsteady sort of relief.

Across from her, Faith sat on the hood of the crashed Jeep with one arm wrapped around her middle, watching the medics work. Jeanne, despite the obvious pain and the bruises already blossoming across her face, had somehow found the energy to lean against a wall and still look composed. Yuki was perched on the edge of the car's passenger seat, one hand pressed to her bad side, looking like she was one shallow breath away from tears and one stubborn act of will away from refusing them. Mei was on a stretcher now, eyes closed, being wheeled toward an ambulance and still somehow breathing, still somehow alive.

Ryo sat beside Rin on the curb, his shoulders slumped, his phone clutched in one hand. He looked as though he'd been hit by a truck, and perhaps he had. He was still pale, still shaky, but there was a small tired smile on his face when he glanced over at her.

"You know," he said, voice raw, "I was going to say I had this all handled."

Rin gave him a sidelong look. "You had a phone and a very lucky moment."

"That's still a plan."

A laugh escaped her despite everything. It sounded broken, but it was real.

The medics came over and started checking them all with clipped competence and practiced calm. One of them looked at Rin's face and said, "You're going to need stitches." Another looked at Ryo and asked if he could stand. He said no, and they said, "That's fine, you're not being graded." It was absurd enough that Rin laughed again.

They were all going to be okay. Maybe not in the way they had hoped, but okay.

When the last of the official noise had thinned out and the ambulances had gone, Faith came over and crouched in front of Rin. Her expression was serious, but not cold. Not anymore.

"You don't have to do this alone anymore," she said.

Rin stared at her. "Do what?"

"Fight this city. Fight this life. Fight the people who keep thinking they can use you."

Rin exhaled slowly. The words settled into the space between them, heavier than they looked.

Faith's eyes flicked to Ryo, then to Yuki, then to Jeanne. "You have people now. People who are willing to stand in the fire with you. That doesn't mean you have to be the only one taking the hits."

The sentence landed harder than Rin expected. She looked down at her hands. They were scraped, knuckles bruised, fingers trembling faintly from the aftershock. She thought of every time she'd told herself to carry it alone. Every time she'd thought if she could just endure one more thing, one more hurt, then she'd be fine. She'd mistaken endurance for independence. And maybe that had been useful. But not anymore.

"I know," she said quietly.

Faith's mouth twitched. Not a smile. Something gentler. "Good. Because you don't have to be the strongest person in the room to make a difference. You just have to be willing to let people help you."

Rin met her eyes. The world around them was still noisy with cleanup and pain and the ordinary wreckage of a night that had nearly ended everything. But in that moment, the weight in her chest shifted. It wasn't gone. It might never be gone. But it had changed shape.

She turned toward the others.

Yuki was trying to look brave and failing in a way that was almost endearing. Mei was awake again, half-propped on the gurney, eyes creased with exhaustion but very much alive. Jeanne had one hand pressed to her side and was pretending not to notice the way her breathing had gone shallow. Ryo looked at her with the same cautious affection he'd worn for weeks now — the kind that was no longer cautious at all.

Rin stood, slowly, and the motion made every bruise in her body protest.

"Come on," she said. "Let's get out of here before somebody decides this lot is a scenic landmark."

That got a tired laugh from Yuki. Even Jeanne's mouth twitched. Faith rose beside her, and Ryo pushed himself up with a grimace and a muttered complaint about how unfair it was that everybody else looked like a disaster while he looked like a cautionary tale.

They started down the street together, battered and sore, but walking.

And for the first time in a very long time, Rin didn't feel like she was walking into the next fight alone.

The city kept moving around them, indifferent and loud and bright with morning. It wasn't peaceful. It wasn't clean. It wasn't over.

But the night had ended.

And for now, that was enough.
