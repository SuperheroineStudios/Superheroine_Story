Chapter Twenty-Eight — The Devil's Weapon

The sky was going gray toward dawn when Ryo's car rocked to a stop at the edge of the park.

"There — she's there." Mei had the door open before the wheels stopped, pointing across the grass to where a silver-haired shape was propped against a tree, upright, moving. Alive.

Yuki limped over and half-fell into the back seat, one arm clamped to her side, and blinked at the girl already sitting there. "Mei? You brought — okay, hi, this is a whole rescue committee now—" She managed a weak grin despite everything. "Ryo, did you finally let a woman in your car voluntarily, or did she—"

"Be serious." Ryo's voice came out clipped. He twisted around to look at her wound, jaw tight. "How bad."

"Bad-ish. Getting better. The endurance thing's real handy, turns out." Yuki waved him off, then her face went grim. "I already called Faith. She's on her way to the compound — that's where Rin went, the Viper's driving her, they're chasing Kyoya. That's where this ends, one way or the other."

Ryo sat with both hands on the wheel and felt the sick familiar weight of it settle over him. Second time. Second time in a handful of days he was about to drive the people he — the people he cared about — straight toward a place designed to kill them. He looked at Yuki, bleeding in his back seat, and Mei, soft and unarmed beside her, and the words maybe I take you both back to the dorm first formed in his mouth—

"Don't," Mei said, before he got them out. Quiet. Certain. "That's not your call, Ryo."

"It really isn't," Yuki agreed.

"You want to go help her. It's written all over you — it's always written all over you." Mei met his eyes in the mirror, and there was no argument to be had in her face, only resolve. "So we go. Now. All of us. She'd already be coming for any one of us and you know it."

Ryo held her gaze one heartbeat longer. Then the hesitation went out of him, the way it always did in the end when it came to Rin, and he faced front and put the car in gear.

"Hold on," he said, and drove.

Rin walked toward Kyoya across the broken lot, and he watched her come like a man watching the tide.

She'd recovered. That was the thing she felt in every step — the shoulder wound already closed to a tight ache, the shakiness gone, the genesis endurance she'd been building for weeks holding her up strong and steady. And Kyoya hadn't recovered at all. He knelt in his own blood where he'd fallen, gut torn, gray-faced, and looked up at the girl he'd hunted across a city, and Rin could see the exact moment he understood he stood no chance.

Jeanne strolled up a few paces behind, unhurried, watching.

"Jeanne." Kyoya's voice cracked. "Jeanne, help me. Get her off me, that's an — I'm still your boss, I'm ordering you—"

"Mm." Jeanne examined her nails. "Funny thing about that. I seem to have misplaced my sense of obligation somewhere back on the highway. Terrible timing." She smiled, thin and cold. "You should really look after your things better, Kyoya."

Kyoya bared his teeth and dragged himself around, slamming a bloody hand against the compound door until it groaned open. "Men!" he bellowed into the dark inside. "To me! Now! Kill them, kill them both—!"

A few faces appeared in the doorway. They took in the scene — the boss on his knees in a spreading dark pool, the small girl and the Viper standing over him — and made the smart choice: they turned and ran, footsteps fading into the building. But a handful didn't. A small, loyal, stupid few came out and put themselves between Kyoya and Rin, weapons up, jaws set.

Jeanne stepped up level with Rin, rolling her shoulders, and the two of them settled into their stances side by side — the veteran and the girl, green threading one's hair and pink cooling in the other's eyes. Jeanne glanced sideways at her, something almost like a grin tugging at her mouth.

"Try to keep up," Jeanne said.

"You try to keep up," Rin answered.

The thugs came at them.

And it wasn't close. Rin moved through them like the weeks of training had rebuilt her from the ground up, because they had — she took the loyal few one at a time, and their swings, the heavy committed blows from Kyoya's strongest remaining men, landed on her guard like nothing. Blows that a month ago would have rattled her skull, sent the world underwater, put her on her back in a dead lot — she blocked them now and felt only the dull thud of impact, her endurance swallowing what would once have broken her. She could have let them land and she'd still win.

Beside her Jeanne was a green blur, fast and surgical, dropping men before they finished their approach. They fought back to back and side by side, and Rin felt the strange rightness of it — fighting with someone at this tier, both of them overpowering the room.

Rin caught the last three in a cluster and drove a high kick into the nearest, sending him crashing into the other two, all three slamming into the compound wall and sliding down in a heap.

She brought her leg down slowly.

And looked at Kyoya.

He was barely upright now, propped against the doorframe, blood-soaked and swaying, but his eyes found hers and held — defiant, hateful, ready. His loyal few were down. His empire was scattering into the dark behind him. There was nothing left between them.

I need to end it. The thought arrived cold and clear. He'd hunted her, cut her friends, kept coming and coming, and he would keep coming — she had no illusions. A man like this didn't stop. The only way he stopped was if she stopped him, here, now, permanently. It would be so easy. One strike. One.

Jeanne watched her, and didn't say a word. She'd spent a lifetime watching people get exactly what they had coming, and she wouldn't lift a finger to stop Rin doing it now.

Rin walked the last steps to Kyoya. She drew her fist back.

Faith's sedan screeched to a halt at the lot's edge behind her. Kyoya lifted his chin, staring death in the face, ready for it. And out past the fence, in the gray dawn light, a blonde figure stood watching with red eyes and held breath — the devil, waiting for the finish she'd spent this whole game engineering. Waiting for Rin to do the one thing that would make her into what Liliana wanted: a weapon, blooded, sharpened, hers.

Rin felt that gaze land on her back.

And stopped.

No.

She wasn't going to do it. Not because Kyoya didn't deserve it — maybe he did — but because she could feel the shape of the trap now, feel the devil out there hoping, and she would not be sculpted into a killer for a monster's amusement. She'd chosen, in a park an hour ago, that she didn't crave the violence anymore. She wasn't going to let anyone — not Kyoya, not a devil in the dawn — decide who she became.

Her fist came around. But not to kill.

It caught Kyoya clean across the jaw, a single hard, decisive blow, and his eyes rolled back and his knees gave and he crumpled into an unconscious heap in his own doorway. Down. Beaten. Alive.

Rin lowered her hand and turned, and looked out past the fence to where the devil stood.

Even at that distance she saw it — Liliana's small pleasant smile faltering into something almost like disappointment, a collector denied the piece she'd wanted. The red eyes held Rin's for one long moment. Then the blonde figure dissolved into a curl of dark smoke and was gone, taking her displeasure with her.

Faith reached them at a run, and pulled up short, taking in the whole tableau — the scattered unconscious men, the fallen crime boss, the girl standing over him with pink eyes and a closed fist. She glanced at Jeanne. Jeanne raised both hands, open and easy, not here to fight, and Faith read it and let it be.

Then Faith looked back at Rin, and at what she'd done, and something moved across her composed face that Rin had never quite seen there before.

"You—" Faith exhaled. "Do you understand what you just did? Kyoya's ring. His people, his compound, him. You just took down the biggest thing crawling through this city." A slow, genuine, almost awed smile. "I've been chipping at the edges of this for months. You walked in and ended it in a night." She shook her head. "I'm impressed, Rin. Genuinely. And — proud. I didn't expect to be, but I am."

Rin looked down at Kyoya, unconscious and small, and didn't feel the triumph she was supposed to.

Because Liliana's gaze was still crawling on her skin, even gone. That disappointed little smile. The way she'd flickered away instead of raging — like a woman who'd lost a hand but not the game. Rin had refused to be the devil's weapon tonight. But some cold certainty in her gut said the devil had other pieces on the board, and that this — whatever this had really been — wasn't over.

Not even close.

Ryo stopped the car a careful distance from the compound, in the lee of a building, and killed the engine.

"It's done," Yuki breathed from the back, ears swiveling, eyes half-closed as she listened to things the others couldn't. "It's — I can hear it. Kyoya's down. The thugs are down. It's quiet over there. Rin did it." A shaky, relieved laugh. "She actually did it."

Ryo let out a breath he felt like he'd been holding since the phone rang. Relief, pure and simple, loosening something in his chest. They'd made it. She'd won. He popped his door and stepped out into the gray dawn, and Mei and Yuki climbed out after him, already starting across the open ground toward the distant figures at the compound.

Ryo took one step to follow.

And stopped.

Something was wrong. He couldn't have said how he knew — it was the thing that lived under his slouch, the mind that ran three steps ahead of everyone else's, the quiet radar that had always told him the shape of a situation before anyone else caught up. And every wire of it was screaming now. Someone was watching them. Watching him. A weight of attention pressing down on the back of his neck like a hand.

He turned, scanning the lot, the shadows, the road. Nothing.

Slowly — dread pooling cold in his stomach — he looked up.

And every thought in his fast, clever head went white and silent all at once.

She stood atop the three-story building beside them, at its very edge — that unmistakable seven-foot frame silhouetted against the graying sky, made monstrous by the height, looking down at Ryo like a woman studying insects on a windowsill. Wild dark hair. A form-fitting purple that left no doubt about the sheer coiled power of her. And her eyes — piercing, pale, lilac, and fixed directly, unmistakably, on Ryo — over a slow, spreading, menacing smile.

Ryo's blood turned to ice.

Amanda had come to play.
