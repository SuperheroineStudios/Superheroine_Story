Chapter Eighteen — The Truth, and the Hat

Rin came back to the world in pieces.

The first piece was motion — the low sway and hum of a car, a seat under her cheek, the smell of cheap upholstery and, underneath it, something distinctly worse that she'd place in a minute. The second piece was pain, everywhere, a deep full-body ache like she'd been folded up and thrown down a flight of stairs, which, she remembered dimly, was not far from the truth. The third piece was her wrists — still bound in front of her, the steel cold and unforgiving.

She stirred, and groaned, and the sound of it turned two heads.

"She's up—! Ryo, she's up!" Yuki twisted around in the front seat, ears springing forward, and the relief on her face was so total and so undignified that Rin almost laughed, except laughing hurt. "Oh thank god, you scared the — do you know how long you were out?"

In the driver's seat, Ryo's shoulders came down from around his ears for what looked like the first time in hours. He didn't say anything flowery — that wasn't him — but he glanced back once, and the look on his face said everything he wouldn't. You're alive. Okay. Okay.

"You're a hard person to keep breathing," he said, eyes back on the road. "Anybody ever tell you that."

Rin worked her dry mouth. Fragments were coming back now, sharp and out of order. The window. The long wrong drop. And before that — a room, and a man with a blade, and then —

Cold flooded her. The woman. The enormous woman with the lilac eyes, lifting her off the floor one-handed like she weighed nothing, the boredom in that pale gaze as she decided Rin wasn't worth the room. Rin had faced a lot of frightening things in the dark. She had never, until today, met one that made every instinct she owned lie down and go silent.

"There was a—" she started, and her voice cracked.

"Later." Ryo's tone was gentle but it left no room. "Whatever it is, save it. Rest till we're back. You can tell it all once, to everybody, when you're not held together with duct tape and luck."

Rin let her head fall back against the seat. For once, she didn't argue.

They pulled in on a quiet service road near the edge of campus, out of sight of the main gate, and the second the car stopped Yuki was around to Rin's door, easing it open, sliding an arm under hers to help her out. Rin's legs held, barely. Ryo hovered close on the other side, ready to catch.

And then there was a blur of cardigan and dark hair, and Mei hit her.

"RIN—" Mei wrapped around her, careful and desperate at once, arms locking gentle across Rin's back, face pressed into her shoulder. "You're okay, you're — I saw them put you in that car, I've been — Ryo texted you were hurt and I didn't know how bad and I—" The words dissolved. She just held on.

Rin, aching everywhere, filthy, exhausted, leaned into her. Let herself lean. Mei was warm and solid and shaking a little, and for a moment Rin closed her eyes and simply rested her weight against the one soft thing that had come running.

"She fell three stories, Mei," Ryo said, dry, from a step away. "Maybe don't crush the ribs."

Mei sprang back, horrified. "Three — three stories? Oh my god, you need a hospital, we need to get her to a—"

"No hospital," Rin said.

"Rin, you fell out of a building—"

"No hospital." Rin sighed. Because a hospital meant questions she couldn't answer, X-rays that would show a body doing things bodies didn't do, and a paper trail into a life that had to stay in the dark. And because — the thought settled over her, tired and certain — she was done. Done keeping this particular door shut against this particular person. Mei had run a mile on grief and thrown herself into a rescue she didn't understand. She'd earned the truth. All of it, soon.

A fourth figure came up the path at an unhurried walk, red catching the last of the daylight. Faith.

"You're awake," Faith said, looking Rin over with that appraising calm. "Good. You have an inconvenient habit of surviving things you shouldn't. Don't lose it." She stopped in front of Rin and nodded at the cuffs. "Hands. Hold them out."

Rin lifted her bound wrists.

Faith brought the edge of her hand down across the short chain between the cuffs in one clean, unhurried motion — and the steel simply broke, the links parting like they were made of chalk, the cuffs falling away from Rin's wrists to clatter on the ground in two ruined halves.

Beside her, Mei made a small strangled sound. "That's — those are metal. That's real metal. How did you—" She looked at Faith with wide, recalibrating eyes. "I really underestimated how strong you are. By a lot. By a whole lot."

"Most people do," Faith said, mild. "It's useful."

Rin brought her freed hands up and turned them over, rolling her wrists, feeling the blood come back. Free. She flexed her fingers and took a breath that didn't have a chain pulling at it, and something in her chest unknotted.

Faith's eyes narrowed slightly, studying her. "You've mended more than you should have already," she observed, quiet enough that only Rin caught the weight of it. "A few hours ago you should've looked like something scraped off a road. Now you just look like someone who lost a fight in a dumpster." A pause. "We'll talk about that. Another time."

Rin looked down at herself — the ruined dress, the grime, the cuts already closed to thin pink lines that should have been raw and weeping. She felt it too. The soreness was real and deep, but the damage was fading faster than it had any earthly right, and she was starting to understand that this — whatever this was — had been quietly saving her life all day.

"Well," Mei said, mustering a wobbly smile, visibly deciding to be brave about all of it, "guess we really don't need a hospital, huh."

Rin looked at her. At Mei — scared and trying not to be, holding a crumpled sunhat she'd apparently carried the entire time, standing here in the fading light having chased Rin into a world she had no business in and refusing to leave it.

"Mei." Rin's voice was rough but steady. "I'm going to tell you the truth. I should've a while ago."

Mei went still.

"I'm a bounty hunter. That's what I do at night — the 'stocking shelves,' the falling boxes, all of it. I hunt people the law can't or won't touch, for money, through brokers. I fight bad people, and I'm good at it, better than I should be." She held Mei's eyes. "And lately I'm finding out there's more to me than I understood. My body does things it shouldn't. I don't have all of it figured out yet. I'm learning what I am a day at a time. But you deserved to hear it from me instead of guessing. So. That's the truth."

For a long moment Mei just looked at her.

Then she nodded, slowly, and the wobble firmed into something warmer. "Okay," she said. "Yeah. Honestly? That — makes a lot of sense. The bruises. The window schedule. The way you move." A small, watery laugh. "I kind of already knew you were something. I just didn't have the word for it. It doesn't change anything, Rin. You're still you."

Rin hadn't realized how tightly she'd been braced for the opposite until it didn't come.

"For the record," Yuki announced, throwing an arm across Rin's shoulders, careful of the soreness, "she's my favorite sidekick. We're a duo. I do the talking, she does the elbows."

"Sidekick," Ryo repeated flatly.

"Nobody believes that, Yuki," Mei said. "Not even a little."

"You're all so rude to me—" Yuki huffed, ears flicking.

The lightness helped. But Rin's face settled into something more serious as the day caught up with her, and she looked around the little circle of them.

"There was a woman," she said. "In the building. Not Kyoya — above him. Enormous. Purple. She walked in and Kyoya practically flattened himself, and she looked at me like I was garbage and threw me out a window without trying. From the way they all moved around her—" Rin shook her head. "I think she runs the whole thing. The whole crime ring. Everyone under her is just… furniture."

Faith frowned — a real frown, a rare crack in the composure. "Purple. Enormous. Runs the ring." She turned it over. "I've spent months working my way up that chain, and I've never heard so much as a whisper of a woman like that. Which shouldn't be possible. Someone with that much weight leaves marks. Names get spoken."

"Maybe that's the point," Ryo said. He'd been quiet, leaning on the car, but his mind had clearly been running. "Nobody talks about her because nobody who sees her walks away to talk. She was ruthless enough to throw Rin out a third-floor window on a whim. Maybe that's just what she does to anyone who lays eyes on her." He glanced at Rin. "She just didn't figure THIS girl would get back up."

"Which means Rin is one of a very short list of people who's seen her and lived," Faith said, and the weight of that settled over the group. "That's worth something. And it's worth being very careful about."

Yuki shifted, ears flicking, like she'd been holding onto something. "Okay — one more weird thing. On the way out, the Viper stepped right in front of the car. Blocked us cold; And then she just… moved aside. Let us go. Smiled about it." She frowned, hugging her arms. "Why would one of Kyoya's people let us walk out with Rin?"

For half a second Faith went very still — there and gone. "Because the Viper doesn't do anything by accident," she said, and there was an old edge under the calm none of them could place. "If she let you live, it served something she wants. That's its own problem, for another day."

"Thank you," Rin said. "For coming. For helping them."

"Don't thank me too fast." Faith's mouth curved, dry. "You should know this one—" she tipped her head toward Ryo "—was about thirty seconds from driving straight through the front gate to take on every green-haired man in that building by himself. I had to block the road with my own car to stop him."

Ryo looked, very deliberately, at a spot on the ground somewhere to his left.

Rin smiled at him anyway. The real kind.

"Big softy," Yuki said fondly, and bumped him with her shoulder, and Ryo let her.

Faith left the way she always did — a turn, red folding into the dusk.

The rest of them went back onto campus together, slow, matching Rin's battered pace. At the fork toward the dorms Ryo peeled off, exhaustion finally showing in the set of his shoulders now that the emergency had passed and Rin was upright and safe.

"Get some sleep," he told her. "All of you. And Rin—" he paused, and whatever he'd meant to say he apparently decided didn't need saying, because he just nodded, once, and it carried the whole weight of the day. "Night."

"Night, Ryo."

Then it was the three of them, and Mei took Rin's hand to steady her up the path toward West Dorm, lacing their fingers, warm and sure.

A beat later, without a word, Yuki took her other hand.

Rin glanced at her. Yuki looked studiously ahead, chin up, ears betraying nothing, absolutely refusing to acknowledge that she'd just claimed the remaining hand purely so Mei wouldn't have both — a silent, stubborn draw in a game neither of them would admit they were playing.

Rin, too tired and too sore and too quietly full to fight it, let them both hold on, and walked back to the dorm flanked by warmth on either side.

"Okay," Yuki declared as they reached the steps, wrinkling her nose theatrically, "first order of business, and I say this with love — let's get you out of that ridiculous dress. You've got dumpster in places a person shouldn't have dumpster. Favorite sidekick or not, you reek."

And Rin — alive, unmasked, held — laughed, and it only hurt a little.
