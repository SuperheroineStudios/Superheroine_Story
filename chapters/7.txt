Chapter Seven — The Long Walk Back

Kyoya did not raise the blade. He didn't need to, and somehow that was worse.

He stood under the dead awning turning the long knife idly in his fingers, and the ease of it told Rin everything her still-ringing head could process. She'd fought his men. She'd fought them and won, and they were competent, dangerous in their own cheap way. This was not that. Whatever animal instinct she'd watched wake in those three thugs when she crossed the street — the recognition of a real thing — she felt it now in herself, looking at him. He carried himself like someone who had never once had to try his hardest, and had stopped wondering long ago whether he'd need to.

His flat bright eyes moved across the scene, taking a slow inventory, and she watched him tally it the way she tallied rooms.

"Let's see." The off-kilter smile stayed fixed. "One of you can barely stand—" his gaze flicked to Rin, and lingered, "—one's running on fumes—" to Yuki, "—and one of you is actually a problem." Back to Faith, a small mocking bow of the head. "The math's not flattering, Lotus. You've got a bad hand and two chips you can't afford to lose."

Faith stood in front of both of them without seeming to move to do it. Her composure hadn't cracked, but Rin — who was learning her tells the way she'd once learned Ryo's — could read the strain underneath, the tightness of a person doing arithmetic they didn't like the answer to. Faith was worried. Not for herself.

Beside Rin, Yuki was swaying with exhaustion, ears flattened, breath ragged from her fight. And still — Rin felt it before she saw it — Yuki shifted her weight and stepped in front of her. Put her small gassed body between Rin and the man with the sword.

"Yuki.." Rin started, or something like it, the word not fully formed.

"Shut up, you can't even stand," Yuki whispered fiercely, not turning around.

Faith's mind was working the same problem Rin's was, faster and colder. Neither of the girls would survive ten seconds against Kyoya as they were. And one-on-one, unencumbered — Faith herself wasn't certain. That uncertainty was the whole game, and Kyoya, for all his manic edge, was not a man who gambled when he didn't have to.

"Here's a thought," he said, and the blade went still. "A truce."

Faith's stillness deepened. Rin saw her expect the trap, saw her scan for it.

"You're waiting for the lie." Kyoya sounded almost pleased. "There isn't one, which I know makes it harder to trust, so let me be boring and explain myself." He nudged one of his unconscious men with the toe of a scuffed dress shoe. "Good help is expensive and getting worse. I can't keep bleeding bodies to whatever this little arrangement of yours is. So. You three walk away. You don't call in whatever cleanup cavalry you'd normally summon, Lotus. I wake my boys up, I carry them home, everybody's inventory stays intact." The smile tilted. "And I don't have to open your two new friends up in front of you, which — believe it or not — I'd consider a professional courtesy. We all leave with what we came for. Isn't that civilized?"

"No," Rin said. The word came out thick, past a jaw that wasn't working right, but it came out. "I want to fight you."

Yuki's head whipped around, eyes wide, an expression that said are you insane louder than any word could.

"You can't," Faith said flatly, not unkindly, without looking away from Kyoya. "You can barely track him with your eyes right now. You'd be dead before your fist closed." She let a breath out through her nose. "He's right. That's the part I hate." Her chin lifted a fraction, addressing Kyoya directly. "We'll go. Truce. But understand — the next time we do this, you won't get to pick the terms."

Kyoya spread his free hand, gracious and disappointed at once. "Next time. Everyone always promises me a next time." He sighed, a genuine little exhale of a thwarted craving. "I did rather hope tonight would have some color to it. It's been a slow week. Ah, well."

His eyes went back to Rin. Held there. Longer than they had any business holding.

"This one, especially," he murmured, and something hungry moved behind the flat brightness, an appetite that had nothing to do with business. "You fight like you want it. I'd have liked to see what's inside someone who fights like that." He tilted his head, studying her the way a collector studies a piece he's decided he'll own eventually. "Some other night, then. I'll keep the blade sharp for you specifically."

Rin's feet moved. She didn't fully decide it — her body just started toward him, past the ringing and the tilt of the world, drawn to the fight the way she was always drawn to the fight —

A hand caught her arm. Yuki's. It closed around her forearm and then slid down and gripped her hand outright, fingers lacing tight, an anchor thrown around a person walking off a ledge.

"No," Yuki breathed. "Not tonight. Please."

Kyoya laughed — a light, delighted, genuinely amused sound that was somehow the most unsettling thing he'd done all night. "Oh, I like her, keep her around." He turned, already losing interest, already crouching by the nearest of his fallen men. "Go on. Before I remember I'm impulsive."

And that was that.

The trio left. Empty-handed, unpaid, the mark abandoned on the curb with the men who'd guard him again by morning. Faith had chosen protection over the fight, and Rin was too rattled to argue and too proud to say she was grateful, and so no one said anything at all as they put distance between themselves and the dead awning and the man humming to himself as he gathered up his broken employees.

They walked a long time before anyone spoke, and Yuki did not let go of Rin's hand the whole way.

"Why did you let him go," Rin said finally, when the ringing had faded enough to leave room for the anger underneath. It came out at Faith, but it wasn't really at Faith. "We had numbers. Three to one."

"We had one fighter and two casualties," Faith corrected, evenly. "Numbers only count when the pieces can move. Yours couldn't." She glanced sideways, and the evenness softened. "I don't lose people I've decided to keep, Rin. Not to salvage a bounty. Not to prove a point to a man who wants us to prove points." A pause. "I didn't want to see you die on a curb over a paycheck. Walking was the only door that didn't end that way."

"I wasn't going to die."

"You were," Yuki said quietly, from Rin's other side, and her grip tightened. "I've never — Rin, I watched you take that hit. Your eyes went somewhere. For a second you weren't in there. And then you tried to walk at the guy with the sword." Her voice wobbled and steadied. "I didn't want to see that. I really didn't want to see that. So I wasn't letting go. That's — that's the whole reason I haven't let go."

Rin was quiet.

The anger had somewhere to go and then, abruptly, nowhere. Because her head was clear now, clear enough to run the honest version of the last twenty minutes — the jaw shot, the world tilting, the way her legs had made decisions without her. Clear enough to know that if she'd stepped toward Kyoya, if Yuki hadn't laced their fingers together and refused, she would very likely be a stain under a broken awning.

"…Yeah," she admitted, at last. "Okay. I might have." It cost her, and they both let it cost her without comment, which she appreciated more than she'd have said. "My head's clear now. Wasn't, then." A beat. "You made the right call."

Faith's mouth curved, small and real. "It's admirable, you know. That you want to walk straight up to the worst thing in the room and take it head-on. Most people spend their whole lives learning to run the other way; you were apparently born pointed at the fire." The curve turned wry. "But admirable and smart aren't the same word. There's a better way to do this work, and it isn't the brave way. Finish fast. Never give a fight room to breathe. And that thing you do — where you've won and you stay, because you like the winning — kill it. It's the most expensive habit you have. It nearly bought you a very short career tonight."

Rin thought of the exact same critique in different words two nights ago, and had the grace, this time, to sit with it instead of arguing.

They reached a corner where the city split three ways, and Faith stopped.

"This is me." She looked them both over — the tired cat girl, the bruised girl — with something almost maternal buried under the composure. "Get her patched up. Sleep. There'll be other jobs; there are always other jobs." A last, warmer glance at Rin. "And I meant it. We'll cross paths again. Try to still be alive for it."

She stepped back, red folding into the dark, and was gone between one breath and the next, the way she left everywhere.

Rin and Yuki walked the rest of the way toward campus with the particular flatness of a night that hadn't gone the way it should have.

"For the record," Yuki said, after a while, nudging her, "I wasn't defeated. I won my fight. Clean. Guard up on the third kick and everything." She lifted her chin, dredging the bounce back up out of sheer will. "So technically the team went one-for-three and the one was me. I'm carrying this operation."

"You're insufferable."

"I'm victorious. There's a difference."

Rin huffed, but it didn't have much bite. Her mind had snagged on something and wouldn't let go — the strangeness of it, the fist coming around and a man being thrown across a street, and her standing there having been pulled out. Saved. She turned the word over and found she couldn't remember the last time it applied. Years. It had been years since anyone had reached into a fight of hers and hauled her clear, and the last time —

The last time it had been Ryo. A long time ago, when they were younger and dumber and she'd bitten off something that would have swallowed her whole, and a slouching boy who claimed not to care about anything had waded in and dragged her out by the collar and never once brought it up again —

"—so I think, and hear me out, I think I should get a codename too, if Faith gets to be the Crimson Lotus then I should be like the Silver something, Silver Fang, no, that's too — Rin? Rin, you're doing a face, are you—"

Rin came back to the street. And realized, all at once, that her hand was still wrapped in Yuki's. Still laced. Neither of them had let go, blocks and blocks ago, well past when the danger had passed.
