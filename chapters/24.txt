Chapter Twenty-Four — All's Fair

Classes crawled by the way good days did — uneventful, ordinary, almost pleasant.

Rin texted Ryo about the spar during first period, one-handed under the desk, and spent the rest of the hour doing what she'd been doing for days now: watching Liliana for a crack. Anything. A slipped word, a red flicker, some tell she could pull on. She got nothing. The professor lectured, took questions, adjusted a student's grip on a pen, and was, in every visible particular, a perfectly normal teacher — which unsettled Rin far more than the vanishing-in-smoke version had. A quiet devil was a devil with a plan.

Vomi's class was just biology, blessedly. And Mei, three seats over, caught Rin's eye halfway through and gave her a small warm smile that had no science in it at all, and Rin felt some tight thing in her chest ease a notch.

When the last bell went, her feet were already carrying her toward the gate.

"Wait up."

Ryo fell in beside her, coffee in hand, unhurried as ever. Rin looked pointedly at the can.

"You've got a gene that could turn you into something out of a nightmare if you pushed it," she said, "and you're using your body to metabolize convenience-store coffee. Ryo. You could be strong."

"I'm strong enough to carry this coffee." He took a sip, serene. "Effort's a slippery slope. You start doing push-ups, next thing you know you care about things." He shrugged. "I didn't come to get recruited into your workout cult. I came to say good luck."

"You trust her now." It wasn't quite a question.

"I trust that she bled for you in that alley and then talked me out of driving into a building instead of letting me get everyone killed. That's more than I trust most people." He tipped the can toward the sedan idling at the curb, red and out of place against the campus stone. "So. Go learn something. Try not to enjoy getting hit too much."

"No promises."

The sedan's window was already down, Faith's ponytail and easy smile visible in the driver's seat. Rin slid in, and they pulled away, and the campus fell behind them.

Ryo watched the car go, took another sip of coffee, and did not hear Yuki come up behind him until she was already there.

"She's got a date," Yuki singsonged, appearing at his shoulder close enough that he flinched. "A violent one, but still."

Ryo took a careful half-step sideways, reestablishing the buffer of personal space he liked to keep between himself and the entire concept of Yuki Kisaragi. He still wasn't used to it — the way she just arrived, all warmth and no regard for the polite distances other people kept.

"It's training," he said.

"It's training with a very pretty woman." Yuki grinned up at him, then let it soften, just slightly, into something more knowing. "Relax, grumpy. I'm not going to bite you." A beat. "I know you only got eyes for Rin anyway. It's painfully obvious. You get this whole face."

Ryo looked at the middle distance.

He didn't confirm it. He also, notably, didn't deny it — just took another sip of coffee and let the silence sit there being an answer, and Yuki, to her credit, let him have it, bumping his arm once and looking off toward the gate with an expression he didn't quite catch.

"Yeah," she said, lightly. "Figured. C'mon, coffee cult. Walk me to the dining hall."

The hill was somewhere past the edge of the city proper — a clearing at the top, grass gone gold in the late sun, the whole sprawl laid out hazy below and not another soul in sight.

"I picked it for the privacy," Faith said, stepping out into the middle of the clearing and rolling her shoulders. "No brokers, no crews, no one to see a teacher throw a student around a field." She glanced back at Rin, a glint in her eye. "Honestly, it'd be quite romantic, if it weren't about to get slightly violent."

Rin was still taking in the view — the gold light, the quiet, the city small and harmless from up here — when the word violent caught up with her, and she turned to find Faith already settling into a loose, ready stance.

"Guard up," Faith said, and struck.

The first flurry came fast but clean — testing strikes, hand after hand, and Rin gave ground and read them and slipped each one, the old fight-sense doing its work. A kick came low and she caught it on a crossed forearm, turned it aside. Not bad, she thought, and pressed.

She advanced. Threw a straight, a hook, drove a kick up the center — and Faith blocked, checked, absorbed all of it with an economy that made Rin's clean technique look busy. So Rin loaded up, planted, and went for a jumping spin kick, committing hard, everything behind it—

And Faith simply wasn't in its path.

Rin felt the blur of it more than saw it — the woman moving with that impossible, frame-skipping speed, out of the arc and behind the beat of it, and before Rin's foot had even finished its useless sweep through empty air, a push kick caught her square and sent her flying back. She hit the grass on her shoulders, skidded—

—and kipped straight back up onto her feet, already squared, barely winded.

Faith paused. And smiled, genuinely impressed. "Most people stay down after that. You forced me to use my speed. Do you know how rarely a spar makes me pull that out?"

"It's a genesis ability," Rin said, settling her stance again.

Faith blinked. "A what?"

"Your speed. It's a genesis ability." Rin watched her react. "It has a name. There's a whole — it's a thing you can understand. I've been learning about it. Working on mine."

Something shifted in Faith's face — a rare, real surprise cracking the composure. "I never had a name for it," she admitted, slower now. "It's just… always been there. Since I was young. I ran track — I was fast, faster than anyone, faster than made sense, and I never questioned where it came from. It was simply mine." She tilted her head, that appraising look returning, sharpened. "And you're telling me it's a — that there's a science to it."

"There is. I'll tell you after, if you can catch your breath." Rin allowed herself the smallest smirk. "Come on. You started this."

Faith laughed, delighted, and came again.

This exchange was better — a real one. They traded a blow each, Rin taking one to the ribs and landing one to Faith's shoulder. Faith swept a kick and Rin deflected it clean. Faith's hand darted out in a fast strike aimed at her collar—

—and Rin caught the wrist.

She moved on pure instinct, the way she'd taken apart bigger opponents her whole life: rolled the caught wrist, stepped in, and torqued Faith's arm up behind her back into a clean, locked hold, chest to shoulder blades, the veteran pinned.

Faith gasped.

And then — Rin felt the whole dynamic change against her back, and it had nothing to do with the fight.

Faith didn't struggle. She leaned into it, going soft and pliant in the hold instead of tense, tipping her head back against Rin's shoulder with a slow, theatrical helplessness. "Oh no," she murmured, entirely unbothered, a smile in her voice. "You've got me. Completely at your mercy." And her free hand drifted — casual, deliberate — back and down, coming to rest warm against the outside of Rin's thigh, fingertips trailing a slow, distracting line.

Rin's brain short-circuited for exactly half a second.

It was half a second too long. Her grip loosened — barely, involuntarily, her composure cracking in a way it never did in a real fight — and Faith felt it instantly. In one fluid motion the pinned arm was free, Faith pivoting out of the hold, and a kick thumped into Rin's guard and shoved her stumbling back three steps.

Rin caught her balance, cheeks hot, glaring. "That wasn't fair."

Faith straightened her ponytail, thoroughly pleased with herself. "All's fair," she said sweetly, "in love and war." And she winked. "You'll want to learn that one too. A fight isn't always won with fists — sometimes it's won by making your opponent forget she's in a fight. You went somewhere for half a second there. In a real fight, that half-second is a knife in the ribs." A beat, the grin sharpening. "Consider it a lesson. Free of charge."

"I'm going to remember this."

"I certainly hope so."

They went at it a long time after that — Rin more careful now, refusing to be baited twice, and Faith stopped holding quite so much back. By the time the sun was sinking toward the far rooftops, both of them were spent, chests heaving, hair stuck to their faces, dropping into the cool grass a few feet apart.

And Rin noticed something as she lay there getting her breath back: she wasn't as wrecked as she should have been. The session had been long, brutal even, and where a version of her from a month ago would have been shaking with exhaustion, this Rin was tired but there, her endurance holding under all of it like the foundation Vomi had described. She could have kept going. Not well — but she could have.

"You're strong," Faith said into the quiet, looking up at the darkening sky. "I mean that. Stronger than I gave you credit for when I met you — and I gave you quite a bit of credit then." She turned her head, and the flirtation had gone out of it, replaced by something straighter and more serious. "I've sparred a lot of people. You made me work today. That's rare."

Rin lay in the grass and turned the feeling over — because it was a new one. When Faith had thrown a man across a street in front of her weeks ago, she'd stood at the bottom of an impossible tier, dazed and saved. Today she'd caught the same woman in a hold. Traded blows. Made her use the speed. She didn't feel outclassed anymore. She felt, for the first time, like she was standing on the same ground Faith stood on — near enough to reach out and touch it.

She knew it wasn't the whole truth. Faith had been teaching, not trying to win, and that skipped-frame speed was still a wall Rin had no answer for. But the gap had closed. The genesis training was working, faster than she'd dared hope.

"I need to work on speed," Rin said. "That's the piece I don't have. You went places I couldn't follow."

"Then that's your next foundation." Faith pushed herself up, brushing grass from the red qipao, and offered Rin a hand. "But not tonight. Tonight you earned the rest." She pulled Rin to her feet. "This was a pleasure, Rin Aizawa. Genuinely. I think I learned as much as you did — I came up here to teach a promising kid, and I'm leaving with a name for the thing I've carried my whole life." A small, real smile. "Genesis. Hm."

"I learned a lot too," Rin said. And meant it — the speed to chase, the hold that worked, the half-second that could kill her, the woman who'd handed her all of it on a golden hill.

Faith drove her back as the last light went out of the sky, and let her out at the gate.

"Same time again soon?" Faith asked through the window.

"Yeah." Rin didn't have to think about it. "Soon."

"Good." A wink — softer than the one on the hill. "Try not to think about me too much, it'll ruin your guard."

And she pulled away into the dusk, taillights shrinking down the road, leaving Rin standing at the gate with sore ribs, a head full of new things to work on, and a warmth she wasn't going to examine too closely settling somewhere under all of it.

She went in through the gate and up toward the dorm, and for once the dark held nothing she was afraid of.
