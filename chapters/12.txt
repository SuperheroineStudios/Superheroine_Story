Chapter Twelve — Before Five O'Clock

Morning found Faith Kiryu standing in the gutted mouth of a storage building, watching other people clean up a mess she hadn't been fast enough to prevent.

The block had the wrong kind of quiet for this hour — cordoned, careful, a scatter of officers and a discreet cleanup crew moving through the emptied units with the practiced calm of people who did this more often than any city should require. They gave her a wide berth. They always did. She'd never asked why, exactly; she suspected the answer had less to do with respect than with the particular stillness she carried, the sense some people got, without being able to name it, that she was standing slightly outside the rules the rest of them lived inside.

A pair of them approached to report. She listened without moving, hands folded, and the report was thin: whatever had been stored here was gone, every unit worth opening opened and stripped, the rest left untouched. Professional. Fast. No prints that would matter, no witnesses who'd talk, nothing left behind but the shape of an absence.

"Nothing else noteworthy?" she asked.

"Nothing, ma'am."

Which meant, of course, that the noteworthy part had left in a truck hours ago, and she was reading a story after the last page had been torn out. She was used to that. It was most of the job.

Then an officer came around the corner half-dragging a man by the collar — a rough type, clothes torn, one eye swollen shut, moving with the careful hunch of someone whose ribs were unhappy with him. "Found this one a block over, ma'am. Out cold in a doorway. He's awake now. Won't say much."

The man lifted his good eye, took in the cordon, the officers, the small composed woman at the center of it all, and set his jaw with the last of a defiance that was mostly bravado. "I don't gotta tell you people anything. I don't know nothin'."

"That's all right." Faith crossed to him, unhurried, and crouched down to his level so he'd have to look at her. She let him see her face. She let him place it. "You don't have to know anything. But I imagine you'd like to walk out of tonight in better shape than you walked into it." A small, pleasant tilt of the head. "They call me the Crimson Lotus. You've heard the name, I think. From the look you're giving me, you've heard it recently — perhaps from three friends who had a very hard Friday."

The defiance drained out of his face like water from a cracked cup. Whatever he'd survived the night before, whatever had put that swelling under his eye, the last thing his body wanted was a second helping from the woman the rumors said had put down worse men than him with far less effort.

"It was the Viper," he said, fast, the words tumbling. "Okay? The Viper and her crew. We — a few of us freelancers, we just heard the pickings were easy out here, nobody watching, and we got here first and she just — she came out of nowhere and put us down, all of us, one after the other, like we were nothin'. Then her people took the whole building. That's it. That's all I know, I swear it."

"I believe you." Faith straightened. "Take him in. Get him a medic — a real one." She turned away before the gratitude could reach her.

The Viper.

She let the name settle, cold and familiar, in the quiet room she kept at the back of herself where she did her actual thinking. Formidable was the polite word for it. So the Viper was flying Kyoya's colors now — of course she was; a talent like that never stayed unaffiliated for long, and Kyoya was exactly the kind of man to collect her. Their paths had crossed once before, Faith and that green-eyed thing, and Faith did not enjoy remembering how it had gone. It was one of a very short list of nights she'd walked away from bleeding, having learned the hard limits of her own considerable advantages. She had spent the time since being certain it would not happen that way again.

She would correct that mistake. She was patient about very few things, but she was patient about that.

And then, unbidden, she thought of the girl. Rin — furious and fast and reckless, throwing herself off rooftops for strangers, standing dazed in front of a man with a blade because some hunger in her wouldn't let her step back. Faith had come to this city with a clean plan and a clear target, and the plan had not accounted for a small, sharp, dangerous eighteen-year-old with a fire she couldn't put out. It certainly hadn't accounted for the Viper walking the same streets, or for how much more complicated the whole delicate arrangement became with two powers now circling the same board.

Things were about to get harder. She could feel it the way she felt weather turning.

She pulled her collar straight, and left the officers to their after-the-fact tidying, and went to think about how to keep a girl alive who seemed determined to make it difficult.
