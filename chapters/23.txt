Chapter Twenty-Three — A Nudge in the Dark

Kyoya ran, and the anger ran out of him as he went.

By the second block it had thinned to something sourer — humiliation, mostly, the memory of that kick catching him in the side of the head, the blade leaving his hand, the sheer astonishment of being touched by a schoolgirl and her strays. But even the humiliation had a bright edge to it he couldn't quite let go of. Because she'd been right there. And she was everything he'd hoped — the fire, the fearlessness, the way she'd walked into a fight she should have lost and made it cost him. He could still turn this. He could still make her see it: that a girl who felt alive only at the edge belonged at the edge, at his side, not babysitting a cat and a bleeding investigator. She'd enjoy it. She'd enjoy him. He just had to—

No.

No, damn it. He slowed, chest heaving, and pressed the heel of his hand to his eyes. It was killing him, this — the wanting, the chasing, the way she'd crawled into his skull and set up house. He was down three men in a night. He'd been thrown out of his own ambush. He could feel the whole obsession dragging at him like a stone in deep water, and for the first time a quieter thought surfaced through it: let it go. Hand Amanda her city, take his best people, walk away from the girl and the whole exhausting hunger of it. Be done.

The relief of that thought was so sudden and so total that he almost laughed.

A woman stepped into his path.

Kyoya stopped so hard he stumbled, caught his own feet, and went down to one knee on the wet concrete. He looked up — and there she was. Blonde. Pale. Unhurried. The devil woman who'd handed him the girl's whereabouts and then vanished into smoke, standing in the middle of an empty street like she'd been waiting there all night.

"Giving up so soon?" Liliana's voice was mild, almost kind. "And after all that. She was right in front of you, Kyoya."

"I'm done with her." It came out ragged, more plea than declaration. "It's not — she's not worth what she's costing me. I'll let it go. I'll—"

"Mm." Liliana tilted her head, and something in the air seemed to soften and warm, and when she spoke again her words slid into him smooth as oil into a lock. "But think of what she'll bring you. You saw it tonight — what she is, what she did, and she isn't even finished growing. A weapon like that, at your side?" A slow smile. "That girl doesn't just win you fights. That girl wins you everything. She's how you stop kneeling to Amanda. She's how you take the whole board and put yourself at the head of it." Her eyes caught the streetlight and held it wrong. "And she's lovely, besides. Imagine it. Rin Aizawa at your right hand, the city yours, Amanda a bad memory. You're really going to walk away from that?"

Kyoya knelt in the road and felt the quitting-thought dissolve like it had never been.

He didn't notice the softness in the air. He didn't notice how the exhaustion had lifted, how the sour humiliation had curdled back into hunger, how a resolve he'd been ready to abandon ten seconds ago was suddenly his again, brighter and more certain than before. It felt like clarity. It felt like his own mind returning to its senses. He had no way of knowing a hand had reached into it and turned a key.

"…You're right." He climbed to his feet, and the grin came back, wider than ever, the manic light rekindled behind his eyes. "You're right. I was — god, what was I thinking. She's the whole point. I'll heal up, I'll get more men, and I'll have her. Amanda won't know what hit her." He was already turning, already moving, a man restored to his madness. "Thank you. Really. I don't know why you keep helping me, but — thank you."

He ran back the way he'd come, faster now, fueled again.

Liliana watched him go, and her smile widened into something that had nothing kind in it at all. Kyoya, chasing Rin. Rin, growing stronger with every fight Kyoya threw her into. And somewhere up the chain, an enormous woman in purple who had no idea two of her pieces were being wound up and pointed at her throat.

"Grow, little fire." Liliana murmured to the empty street.
