Chapter Three — First Day

Rin woke before the alarm, which was normal, and hurt more than usual, which was also normal.

She lay still a moment and took the tally the way she took it every morning. The gut shot from last night had settled into a deep, satisfying ache low on her left side — the tattooed man had put real weight behind it, she'd give him that, right before she put an elbow behind his ear. She pressed two fingers against the bruise, felt the tenderness bloom and fade, and decided it was nothing. She'd worn worse to worse places. A bruise was just a receipt.

Across the room, Mei was awake and pretending not to look.

She was very bad at it. She was sitting up in her tangle of quilt and lamps, hair a lost cause, ostensibly reading something on her phone, and her eyes kept flicking to Rin's side where the hem of her shirt had ridden up over the edge of the bruise. When Rin looked over, Mei snapped her gaze back to the phone so fast she nearly dropped it.

"Morning," Mei said, brightly, guiltily.

"Morning."

"I wasn't— " Mei gestured vaguely at the air, at nothing, at the general concept of not staring. "You have a— there's a bruise. On your— I wasn't looking, I just, it's a small room, things are in it, my eyes have to go somewhere." A beat. "Convenience store stocking is dangerous."

"Boxes are heavy," Rin said, pulling her shirt down.

"Boxes did that?"

"Fell off a shelf."

"The box fell, or you—"

"Mei." Rin stood, unhurried, and reached for her uniform. "It's fine."

Mei's mouth pressed into a line, holding a whole balloon of thoughts captive again, and then she let it go with visible effort and pivoted to the far more urgent crisis of it being morning and her being awake during it, which she narrated at length while she fought her hair into something the dress code might tolerate.

They got ready in the small warm choreography of two people learning to share a space — Mei talking, Rin quiet, the sticky note reading HYDRATE finally falling off the wardrobe door for good and getting replaced, immediately, with a fresh one. They compared schedules over the last of Rin's rice. There it was, at the bottom of both columns, the one thing they shared: last period, biology, a name printed in the same warm printer-ink as everything else.

Prof. K. Vomi.

"We have a class together!" Mei lit up like it was a lottery win. "End of the day. Biology. Oh thank god, I won't have to sit alone and do the thing where I pretend I like sitting alone."

"You mentioned the thing."

"It's a very developed thing. I've perfected it." She grinned. "But now I don't have to. See you at the finish line, roomie."



The first class was, to Rin's mild surprise, completely normal.

She sat near the back, by the window, because that was where the exits made the most sense, and she listened to a nervous lecturer explain a syllabus, and nothing tried to hurt her for ninety entire minutes. It was strange. The static hummed under it, the way it hummed under all the ordinary hours, but she found she could sit inside it here — the sun through the glass, the scratch of pens, a hundred and forty girls and a scattering of boys all pretending they weren't nervous. It was almost restful. She could see why people did this instead of the other thing.

At the break she went looking for coffee and found Ryo instead, exactly where she should have expected — leaning on a railing in the courtyard, tie a rumor, coffee can in hand, radiating the specific serenity of a man in a school full of people whose opinions could not reach him.

"You survived a class," he said.

"Barely." She leaned on the railing beside him. "I have to tell you about last night."

His whole posture shifted a fraction — attention under the slouch. "Go."

"So I'm on the roof, and I look down, and there's my mark, except he's not—"

"There you are!"

Yuki arrived like weather. Silver hair, human-eared today — Rin caught herself checking, and caught nothing, which was its own small mystery for later — bag half off one shoulder, cutting across the courtyard on a direct intercept course with a grin already loaded. "I knew you went here, I've been looking all — okay, hi, I'm not interrupting, am I interrupting?" She was absolutely interrupting. "Who's this?"

She rounded on Ryo and stuck out a hand, radiant.

"Yuki Kisaragi! Are you Rin's friend? You're giving off a friend energy. A grumpy friend energy. I like it."

Ryo looked at the offered hand. Rin watched it happen for the second time in two days, the great collapse: the tips of the ears going first, then the jaw setting into that terrible studied casualness, then the handshake that lasted a single unit of time before he let go like it burned.

"Ryo," he said.

"Ryo," Yuki repeated, delighted, filing it somewhere.

"I—" He pushed off the railing, coffee suddenly very interesting. "Rin. The story. Later. I'll get it from you later." And he was walking, unhurried on the surface and not remotely unhurried underneath, off toward anywhere that wasn't here.

Yuki watched him go, head tilting, tail-less today but Rin could have sworn the phantom of one flicked. A slow, wicked understanding spread across her face.

"Men," she announced, with enormous satisfaction, "are so easily manipulated. You just have to smile. It's almost sad. Look at him go."

"Thank you for that," Rin said.

"For chasing off your grumpy friend? Ah." Yuki waved a hand, magnanimous. "Least I can do. You did fold a man in half for me last night. We're square." She leaned on the railing in Ryo's vacated spot, and her voice dropped, conspiratorial, the performance falling away into something realer. "Hey. So. About last night."

"You dropped your guard on the third kick again in your head just now, didn't you."

"I have thought about that kick forty times." Yuki scowled at the courtyard. "But no. Listen. After you did your— " the clumsy spin-mime, incurable " —and left me standing there like an idiot, I stuck around. Kept to the shadows. Real professional, you'd have been proud." She lowered her voice further. "And this woman shows up. With a crew. And they just— took them. The goons. Cleaned the whole thing up like it was nothing." A pause, and something almost reverent crept in. "She was— okay, she was stupidly cool. Like, sexy-badass-in-red cool. Moved like she owned the street. And I was hidden, Rin, I know how to hide—"

"Debatable."

"—I know how to hide, and I swear to god right before they left she looked right at where I was. Just for a second. Like she knew. Like she'd known the whole time." Yuki shivered, ears that weren't there flattening in memory. "Scary. Cool, but scary. I did not stick around after that."

Rin filed it. A woman in red, a crew that swept up Kyoya's people, eyes that found a hidden cat girl in the dark. That wasn't a broker's cleanup. Brokers sent vans and bored men. That was something else, something with intent, and it had been circling the same night she had.

The break-bell went before she could turn it over further.

"Oh—we've got the same next one!" Yuki brightened, the shadow gone as fast as it came. "I checked. Come on, walk with me, you can tell me I dropped my guard some more, I find it weirdly motivating—"



The room went quiet when the professor walked in, and it wasn't the ordinary quiet of a class settling.

She was blonde, tall, and beautiful the way a drawn blade is beautiful — all clean lines and cold light. A black suit jacket over a white blouse, a pointer in one hand that she didn't need and clearly liked, and a way of moving that made a lecture hall feel like a stage she'd been given rather than a room she'd entered. She set her things on the desk, leaned a hip against it, and looked out over the class with pale blue eyes that seemed to catalogue every face and find most of them uninteresting.

"Liliana," she said. "You may skip the professor. We'll be spending enough time together for formality to become tiresome."

Yuki, beside Rin, leaned in. "Okay she's terrifying," she whispered, thrilled.

Liliana taught the way a cat plays — languid until it isn't. She asked questions, and the questions had teeth; they weren't the syllabus kind with answers in the back of a book. They were the kind meant to expose who'd done the reading and who was going to have a hard year. One by one she picked students off, watched them fumble, and moved on with the faint patience of someone waiting for the room to become worth her time.

Then she asked one, offhand, half-turned toward the board, and Rin answered it.

Flatly. Correctly. Without raising her hand or her voice.

Liliana paused. Turned. Looked at her — actually looked, the cataloguing gaze stopping on Rin's face and staying. She asked another, harder, aimed this time. Rin answered that one too. And a third, sharper still, the kind meant to trip, and Rin took it apart in a sentence and a half because it wasn't really that different from reading a room full of men before a fight — you just found the load-bearing thing and put your weight on it.

The lecture hall had gone very still.

"Hm," Liliana said. It was a small sound, but it landed like a door opening somewhere far off. She held Rin's eyes a beat too long, and something moved behind her own — interest, yes, but a specific, appraising, collecting kind of interest, the look of a woman adding a name to a list Rin very much did not want to be on. "What did you say your name was?"

"I didn't."

The faintest curve touched Liliana's mouth. "No," she agreed softly. "You didn't." And she turned back to the board and carried on, and did not call on Rin again, and somehow that was worse.

Beside her, Yuki was staring. "She likes you," she hissed. "That's her liking someone. I've decided. That was so much scarier than her hating you—"

At the bell, Rin gathered her things fast. But at the door, some pull she didn't examine made her glance back, and Liliana was watching her go, chin propped on one hand, that far-off door still open behind her eyes. She didn't smile. She just watched, the way Rin watched rooms, the way the woman in red had apparently watched a hidden girl in an alley — like she already knew how the story ended and was only curious how long Rin would take to get there.

Rin walked out into the hall and let the door swing shut on it.

"Okay bye, that was a lot, I need a snack," Yuki declared, peeling off toward the evil vending machine Mei had warned about. "See you around, Rin! Tell your grumpy friend I said—actually don't, it's funnier if I do it myself—"

And she was gone.



The next break was quiet, and Rin spent it the way she spent quiet — sitting alone on a low wall in the shade, letting the day's odd pieces turn over in the dark behind her eyes. The green streak. The woman in red. Liliana's far-off open door. Too many watchers for one small city.

"So." Ryo dropped onto the wall beside her, out of nowhere, the way he did. "The grumpy friend gets his story. Finally. No cat girl this time?"

"You mean the one who reduced you to a puddle in four seconds."

"I don't know what you're talking about."

"Your ears did a thing."

"My ears do lots of things. They're very active ears." He took a pull of coffee, gloriously unbothered, which meant it had gotten to him more than usual. "Story. Go. Before someone else adopts you."

So she told him. The roof, the mark on the street, the man beside him — and she watched Ryo go still at the description, the tattoo, the single green streak.

"One of Kyoya's," he said quietly. It wasn't a question with him either. "On a public street. Breaking into a pharmacy." He turned the coffee can slowly in his hands, the slacker gone somewhere underneath, that buried steel showing at the surface. "That's wrong. Not wrong like bad—wrong like it doesn't fit. Kyoya's people don't do smash-and-grabs on cold medicine. That's beneath them. So either it wasn't about the pharmacy, or somebody's using his colors who shouldn't be." His jaw set. "And this woman in red. The one your cat girl saw cleaning up after. Crew, coordination, made a hidden kid from the shadows." He shook his head slowly. "That's not a broker. That's a player. Somebody working the same board you are, from a lot higher up."

"I know."

"Do you know, or do you know and not care because it was a good fight."

Rin didn't answer that, which was its own answer.

Ryo exhaled through his nose, a sound she'd taught him or he'd taught her, they'd never worked out which. "You went over a roof for a stranger."

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"You were about to do the look."

"I have no idea what look you—"

"Rin!"

Mei arrived at a jog, bag bouncing, waving with a whole arm. "Last class! We're gonna be late, well, I'm gonna be late, you're never late, it's unnatural—oh! Ryo! Hi again! From the dorm!" She beamed up at him, entirely unaware of the effect. "I didn't scare you off last time, did I? You left kind of fast—"

"You didn't," said Ryo, too quickly, and stood, and this time only his ears betrayed him, and only a little, because Mei's brightness was a different frequency than Yuki's calculated beam. "I've gotta go anyway. Home stuff." He looked at Rin, and there it was after all, the almost-smile, quiet and unbearable. There she is. "Text me. This time actually text me."

"Maybe."

"Rin."

"Maybe."

He went. Mei watched him go, then rounded on Rin with delight. "He did it again! Bolted the second I showed up. Is it me? Be honest—do I have a face that makes grumpy handsome boys flee? Because that's twice now."

"Class, Mei."



The biology room smelled faintly of antiseptic and something colder underneath, and Professor Vomi was already there when they came in, and Rin noticed her the way she noticed threats.

She was striking — tall, taller than Liliana, with long auburn hair spilling over the shoulders of a white lab coat worn open over red and blue, and she moved through the room slowly, deliberately, touching the edge of each lab bench as she passed like she was counting them. When she spoke, her voice was low and smooth, a velvet that carried to the back row without effort and made the ordinary words of a biology syllabus sound like they meant something else.

"Life," she said, by way of beginning, "is mostly a problem of what a body will tolerate. And for how long." She smiled, small and precise. "We'll get to specifics."

She was brilliant, obviously — the lecture was miles above the nervous first one that morning, dense and fast and elegant, ideas laid out like a woman arranging instruments on a tray. And she was calculated, every word placed, every pause deployed. She never fumbled, never softened. Rin found herself sitting very straight, the old animal part of her fully awake, the part that knew which people in a room you never turned your back on.

Beside her, even Mei had gone quiet, scribbling notes, occasionally whispering she's kind of scary-pretty, right, is it just me.

It wasn't just her.

Vomi didn't single Rin out. Didn't ask her questions, didn't do the collecting thing Liliana had done. She simply taught, and let the cold undercurrent do its work on the whole room at once. But at the very end, as the class packed up and the light went amber through the high windows, Rin looked up from her bag — and found Vomi already looking at her.

Just a glance. A single held second across a emptying room. The professor's expression didn't change; there was no smile, no invitation, no far-off open door. Just a flat, patient, measuring look, the look of a woman noting where something is so she can find it again. Then a student asked her a question, and she turned away, velvet voice folding back into the ordinary, and the moment was gone as if it had never happened.

Rin shouldered her bag and told herself it was nothing.

Two professors in one day. Too many watchers for one small city.



The walk back to the dorm was gold and easy, the day cooling off around them, and Mei filled it, which Rin was grateful for, because it meant she didn't have to.

"—and Liliana, everyone was already talking about her at orientation, apparently she's brilliant and she's made three second-years cry and she's only been here a semester, and Vomi, okay, tell me Vomi didn't feel like she could kill you with a slideshow, I got chills, good chills, confusing chills—" Mei bounced a little as she walked, sleeves flopping. "How was your day? You had classes without me. Were they sad and empty. Did you miss me. Say you missed me."

"It was a long day," Rin said, which was true.

"That's not a no." Mei grinned, then softened, bumping their shoulders together lightly as they climbed the steps toward the west dorm and the warm yellow lights coming on in the windows. "I had a good first day. Weird professors and all. Best one I've had in a while, honestly." She glanced sideways, a little shy of her own sincerity again. "'Cause of the roomie. Just so that's on the record."

Rin looked at the girl beside her, the sunlight going out of the sky behind the buildings, the ache of last night still quiet under her ribs, and all the watchers of the day circling somewhere out past the gates.

"Yeah," she said. And found, to her own faint surprise, that she meant it. "It's on the record."

They went inside together, and for one more evening, the other city stayed where it was — out in the dark, waiting, patient as a professor with a list.
