Chapter Twenty-Seven — Run to Ground

The jeep ate the dark road, and Rin bled quietly into the passenger seat.

Less than she had been, at least. The deep line across her back shoulder had slowed to a seep, the genesis in her already knitting the worst of it closed, and she pressed a hand flat against it and breathed through the ache and watched the city smear past the window.

"You've stopped ruining my upholstery," Jeanne observed, eyes on the road. "I appreciate that. Blood's murder to get out of leather."

"Viper—"

"Jeanne." The correction came fast and flat, without heat. "If you're going to be in my car, you can use my name. I've had enough of the other one for one lifetime."

"…Jeanne." Rin turned to study her — the green threading her dark hair, the easy competent hands on the wheel, the woman who'd let her walk out of a lot once and had now screeched to her rescue out of nowhere. "Why are you helping me. Are you helping me. Because from where I'm sitting you've spent this whole time working for the man we're chasing."

"Kyoya needs to fall." Jeanne said it simply, like a fact about weather. "I've spent a long time being the sharpest thing in his hand — his and the one above him. Pointed at whatever they wanted broken, whenever they wanted it. I never much cared for being a puppet." Her jaw tightened, just slightly. "You're the first thing to come through this city in years with a real chance of knocking the whole rotten table over. So yes. I'm helping you. Consider it enlightened self-interest."

Rin let that settle. It was honest, at least — no pretty coat of paint on it.

"And Amanda?" She watched Jeanne's face for the flinch. "If we run him back to the compound, she could be there. We could both die tonight the second we walk through that door."

Jeanne didn't flinch. She just drove on, the streetlights sliding across her green eyes, and said nothing at all — which was, Rin was learning, its own kind of answer.

Ryo sat at his desk being as red as a man could be while a distraught girl perched on the edge of his bed.

It was, he'd decided, a uniquely terrible evening. Mei had turned up an hour ago in a state, because Rin wasn't answering her calls, and Ryo — who kept the concept of other people at a careful arm's length and the concept of crying people at two arm's lengths — had ended up with her sitting in his room, wringing her hands, filling his careful quiet with worry he had no idea how to fix. He was seriously considering getting up and going to look for Rin himself, mostly as a way to get his own room back.

His phone rang.

Mei was on her feet before it finished the first ring. Ryo checked the screen — Yuki — and answered, and Mei crowded in against his shoulder to listen, close enough that he went rigid on pure reflex and then, given the circumstances, decided to let it go.

"Ryo." Yuki's voice came thin and strained, and the slouch went out of him instantly. "I need help. I'm — okay, I'm pretty hurt, I'm not gonna pretend I'm not. And I've gotta tell you something, and you're probably gonna be mad."

"Where's Rin?" Mei blurted into the phone. "Yuki, where is she, is she—"

"Spit it out," Ryo said, low and fast, the steel already surfacing.

A shaky breath on the line. "Rin got in the Viper's jeep. She's — they're chasing Kyoya. He was here, he ambushed us, he's the one who cut me up — but Rin got him good, he's hurt bad, worse than me, and he ran, and she went after him." A pause. "I told her to go, actually. I hope she finishes it."

Ryo was already standing, grabbing his jacket off the chair. There was no debate in him — there never was, when it came to her.

"Where are you," he said. "I'm coming right now."

He rattled off the plan while he moved — location, get Yuki, keep pressure on the wound — and Mei was already at the door ahead of him, jaw set, tears scrubbed off her face and replaced with something harder. There was no version where she stayed behind and he didn't waste breath suggesting it. Whatever was happening out there tonight, it was happening to Rin, and that meant both of them were going.

Kyoya made it back to the compound on a lung full of fire and both hands clamped over his gut.

He didn't know if she'd chase. That was the thing gnawing at him even now, staggering across the cracked lot toward the doors — surely even she wouldn't come for him after that, surely she'd stay with her bleeding cat and let him crawl away to lick his wounds. He just had to get inside. Get patched. Get people around him. He couldn't afford her catching him like this, weak, emptied out, a blade-wound singing in his middle.

Then he saw her.

The blonde. The devil woman, standing at the edge of the lot in the dark, watching him with those wrong red eyes and that small pleasant smile — present at every turn of this, and never once helping. And the sight of her dragged the truth up into the light at last, the thing he'd been too far gone to see.

The obsession had never been his.

That was what broke something in him, kneeling there in the cold. All these weeks he'd been so certain the hunger for the girl was his own — his want, his fixation, his fire reaching for hers. It wasn't. Every time his resolve had cracked, every time he'd stood ready to simply walk away, something had turned him gently back around and set him hunting again, and he'd mistaken it for his own clarity returning. It had been a hand in his head. The devil had never wanted a single thing from him. She'd wanted the girl — Rin, wound up and aimed and sharpened — and he'd been nothing but the whetstone, ridden and steered and hollowed out chasing a craving that had been slipped into him like a hook behind the eye.

He didn't say any of it. What would be the point — she knew, she'd always known, and she'd only smile that patient smile if he spoke. So he stayed silent, kneeling in the cold with the horror of it settling over him, and just looked at her.

The devil woman just smiled, and said nothing, and did not move to save him.

An engine growled across the lot.

Kyoya turned — and there were headlights, a familiar sleek shape braking hard on the broken asphalt, and for one wild desperate heartbeat hope flared in him. Jeanne. Jeanne's jeep. His sharpest tool, his best hand, come to collect him and carry him inside and put bodies between him and the dark. He was saved. His legs gave and he dropped to one knee, spent, grinning up at the car with bloody relief.

The doors opened.

Jeanne stepped out. Good. Yes.

And out the other side, unfolding from the passenger seat with a slash across her shoulder and pink eyes gone hard and cold in the dark, came Rin Aizawa — the last person in the world he wanted to see.

Kyoya's grin died on his face.
