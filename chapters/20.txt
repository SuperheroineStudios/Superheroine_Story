Chapter Twenty — The Genesis Eye

Rin came up from the lecture with a headache and the odd, humming certainty that she'd just been handed the shape of her life.

Vomi had been clinical in the way only the truly certain could manage — not cold, simply unembarrassed by facts. The gene. The dormant expression. The way stress and repetition could push a body past what it had once been capable of. The categories. Endurance. Strength. Speed. Insight. The things people called anomalies because they had no language for them and no desire to learn one.

Vomi's eyes had settled on Rin across the lecture hall as though she had been expecting this particular answer to arrive in a room full of students asking for grades. "What you have," Vomi said, "is not a metaphor. It is a biological capacity. And the capacity can be cultivated. If you want to survive what you're beginning to invite into your life, you will need to learn to use it on purpose."

Rin had nodded, and something in her chest had gone still and bright.

The world had shifted under her, but not in the way she feared. It wasn't that she was becoming something monstrous. It was that she had been carrying a map all along and had finally been told how to read it.

By the time she got back to the dorm, the lecture had been sitting in her like a lit match. She found Yuki in the common room with a look on her face that meant she already knew something was up. Ryo, as usual, was one step behind everyone else's understanding, but the look he gave Rin had the same shape as his questions — the sort that arrived before the words.

And then Mei — sweet, brave, impossible Mei — had looked up at the sight of Rin and done something that made her chest tighten.

She said nothing. She simply crossed the room, sat beside Rin, and stayed there.

That was enough.

For the first time since the window, since the cuffs, since the whole terrible bright mess of the week before, Rin let herself be still for a little while and let other people hold the air around her.
