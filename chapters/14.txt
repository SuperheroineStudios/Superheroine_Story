Chapter Fourteen — Quietly

The sidewalk had gone very quiet, the way a street does when everyone on it has decided at once to pretend they aren't watching.

Rin stopped a few feet from the woman in green and set her weight even, hands loose, reading her the way she read everything that might try to kill her. Up close the Viper was worse — not louder, quieter, a stillness with a spring under it, green eyes bright with an amusement that never quite touched the cold underneath. She looked Rin over head to foot and seemed pleased by what she found.

"You're smaller than the stories," she said. "But the eyes are right. He said the eyes would be right."

He. Rin filed it. Kyoya. So this wasn't a random collision after all.

"There's an easy way to do this," the Viper went on, pleasantly, unhurried. "You come with me. Quietly, on your own two feet, no fuss. I'll even explain the situation on the drive — I'm not unreasonable, whatever you've heard." Her head tilted a fraction toward the café window, toward the small terrified shape pressed against the glass. "Or we do it the other way, and it gets messy right here, on this nice little street, with all these nice people. And your friend gets a much closer look at the sort of afternoon I'm capable of." A soft smile. "I don't think that's what she came out for today. Do you?"

Rin's whole body wanted to move. She could see it, the entire sequence, laid out clean in front of her the way it always was — the step inside, the elbow, the knee, the woman on the ground before the threat finished leaving her mouth. And every cell of her that fed on danger screamed to do exactly that.

But the Viper moved like Faith. Rin had felt that tier from the bottom of it two nights running, dazed and saved. Maybe on her best day, healed and rested and not watched, she could make it a real fight. Not here. Not fast enough to guarantee the woman never got past her to the glass. And even if she could win it — she'd have to become it. Full and terrible, right there in the sunlight, three feet from a window with Mei behind it.

She looked back, once, at the café.

Mei was pressed to the glass, both hands flat against it, mouthing something Rin couldn't hear and didn't need to. Don't. Please. Don't.

There was no version of this where Rin fought and Mei walked away still looking at her the way she'd looked at her on the hill. And there was no version where she stayed silent and this woman didn't come in.

"Fine," Rin said. Flat. Hating every letter of it. "I'll go. Quietly."

"Perfect." The Viper's smile widened, genuine now. "See, this is why he likes you. Smart under all that fire." She stepped back toward the jeep and beckoned. "Come on, then. Close the distance."

Rin walked toward her, surrendered, jaw tight — and the Viper's hand came up with a soft metallic sound, and there was a pair of cuffs dangling from two fingers.

"Ah — one small thing." The green eyes glittered. "We don't want any funny business on the drive, do we, babe. A girl who folds five men in a dead lot doesn't get to ride with her hands free. Nothing personal."

Rin's teeth ground together. But she held out her wrists, because the alternative was still the window, still Mei, and let the cold steel click shut around them. The humiliation of it burned worse than any punch she'd ever eaten — being cuffed, handled, walked to a car like cargo, while the one person she'd sworn on a hillside to protect watched through glass.

The Viper guided her into the passenger seat with one hand on her shoulder, unhurried, and shut the door. Rounded the hood. Slid into the driver's seat in a single fluid motion, engine already turning over.

The last thing Rin saw as the jeep pulled from the curb was the café window — and Mei, both hands still pressed to the glass, her face coming apart.

Then the street pulled away, and the city swallowed them, and Rin Aizawa was gone.
