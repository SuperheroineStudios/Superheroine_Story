Chapter Eight — The Fifth Door

The next morning, the dorm felt smaller than it had the day before.

Rin woke before the alarm and lay still in the half-dark, listening to the room settle around her. Mei's breathing was soft in the bed across from hers. The light from the hallway slipped under the door in a thin strip and made the floorboards look cleaner than they were. The whole place smelled faintly of laundry detergent and old wood and the damp kind of winter that lingered in stone buildings.

She should have felt rested.

She did not.

The night had left a bruise on her jaw and a sharper kind of ache behind it, the kind that came from being reminded, too clearly, that there were people who could step into the middle of her bad decisions and decide they were going to stop them. She hated that. She hated the part of herself that had wanted to see Kyoya, the part that had wanted to prove something to a man who could have ended her life in three breaths. She hated the fact that Faith had been right. She hated, more than anything, how much the memory of Yuki's hand around hers lingered.

It was not a feeling she knew what to do with.

The door opened just as she sat up.

Mei looked in, saw her awake, and smiled in that already-bright way that made the room feel more inhabited than it was. "I made coffee," she announced. "Or at least I boiled whatever was in the machine and called it coffee. It's not great, but it's there. Also, before you ask, you're not going to be brave and pretend you don't want it."

Rin stared at her for a beat. Then, because the room had gone ridiculous and because she was tired and because Mei's voice had the strange property of making the walls feel less like walls, she said, "Fine."

Mei grinned like she'd won a contest and disappeared into the hall. A minute later she returned with a mug and a pastry wrapped in paper. She set them on the desk and climbed onto the bed with a sigh, bare feet tucked up under her, looking perfectly pleased with the world and baffled by its general quality.

"You know," she said, chewing the corner of the pastry, "if you want to keep having mysterious nights and coming back with bruises, I would like to be formally informed that I am now an accessory. I feel like I deserve a title. 'Roommate of the Year' would be appropriate."

Rin took the mug. The coffee was terrible, but warm. "You'd make a terrible accessory."

"I would be excellent. I have emotional support qualities."

"You're impossible."

"You love me."

Rin did not answer that. She took a sip of the coffee, and the taste sat bitter and familiar on her tongue, and for a second she was back in the alley, Yuki's hand in hers, Faith's voice in the dark telling her there were better ways to do this work.

Mei looked at her over the rim of the mug. "You do know you're allowed to be a person today, right?"

That made Rin look up.

"You don't have to be the thing you become when the lights go low. You can be a girl with a bad coffee and a bad sleep and a still-sore jaw and I will feed you pastries and we will both pretend that was a real sentence."

The corner of Rin's mouth moved. Not a smile, not quite. Something softer. "You're weird."

Mei nodded as if this were an observation of great seriousness. "Yes. But in a comforting way."

They sat in the soft gray morning until the bell rang and the corridor filled with noise again. By the time the day had properly begun, Rin had a little space around her thoughts. Not peace, not exactly. But enough room to let the world be ordinary for a few hours.

The ordinary, it turned out, was not to last.

The first clue came during lunch. Rin had been standing outside the cafeteria with her tray and a book she wasn't reading when she caught sight of Yuki halfway across the courtyard. Yuki was moving fast, eyes on the path ahead of her, shoulders tense, and there was a small furrow between her brows that meant trouble.

Rin was halfway to her before she was fully aware of moving.

"Yuki?"

The cat girl stopped so abruptly she nearly tripped over her own feet. She looked up, saw Rin, and the tension in her face sharpened. "Rin."

"You okay?"

Yuki glanced around, then lowered her voice. "Not here."

That was enough.

They found a bench near the old greenhouse, out of view of most of the campus. It smelled faintly of damp soil and old glass. Yuki sat, folded her hands tight in her lap, and didn't look at Rin for a long moment.

"I think someone followed me," she said.

Rin went still.

"No, not like a normal person followed me. Like—" Yuki made a face. "Like they were trying not to be obvious. I kept noticing the same guy around the market, and then again near the train station, and then on the path behind the east dorms. It was like he didn't even care if I saw him. Like he wanted me to notice." She swallowed. "I thought maybe I was being dramatic. But then I saw him at the back of the cafeteria today. He wasn't looking at my face. He was looking at the way I walked. Like he was making a map."

Rin's jaw shifted. "Describe him."

"Tall. Dark coat. Short hair. He had one of those blank faces that makes you feel like you don't matter at all. The sort of person a person would trust if they were trying to be stabbed in a back alley."

Rin already had a mental picture. Too clean. Too controlled. Too much like someone who had been trained to make himself forgettable while still being impossible to ignore.

"Any sign of a marker?"

Yuki shook her head. "Nothing. No tattoo. No symbol. Just a face I can't stop remembering."

Which was, Rin thought, the worst possible kind of warning.

She stood. "Come with me."

Yuki blinked. "Where?"

"Somewhere with fewer eyes." Rin looked at the old greenhouse, the empty courtyard beyond it, the people moving in bright clusters around campus. Then she looked back at Yuki. "We're not going to panic. We're going to be smart. If somebody's following you, they want you to know it. That means they want something. We find out what that is before they get it."

Yuki's mouth trembled, once, at the edges. Then she nodded. "Okay."

They walked without speaking until they reached the roof access stairwell behind the east dorms. Rin climbed first and waited at the top for Yuki to catch up. The city spread below them in a wash of gray and gold, all the places a person could disappear into if they wanted to. It was quiet up there. Clean. The kind of place where a person could hear their own thoughts if they were lucky.

Rin turned and put both hands flat on the railing. "All right. Start from the beginning. Tell me everything you saw. Every detail you can remember."

Yuki did.

By the time she was done, Rin had a picture of a man who had never once overplayed his hand. He'd been close enough to be noticed without ever acting like he belonged there. He'd moved like someone who knew that attention was a weapon, and that when applied correctly, it could turn a person into a target without ever looking like a threat.

"He wasn't following you because he liked you," Rin said. "He was learning your habits. That's worse. It means he doesn't need to track you in the open if he can predict where your body will take you next."

Yuki swallowed. "So what do we do?"

Rin looked across the city. "We make him regret that."

Yuki blinked. "That sounded extremely dramatic."

"It was meant to."

They spent the rest of the afternoon setting the trap.

It was an ugly little piece of work and exactly the sort of thing Rin liked. Not because it was clever — though it was — but because it had shape. It had edges. It had a point. They chose the old station entrance near the east edge of campus, the place where the concrete was cracked and the shadows were deep and the city lights thinned. They waited under the dim overhang until the evening had fully turned.

And then the man came.

He wasn't hasty. He walked as though he belonged to the street, shoulders relaxed, hands in the pockets of a dark coat. When he reached the station entrance, he slowed, looked once toward the road, and then, with no visible change in expression, turned toward the alley beside the building.

Rin nodded once.

Yuki tensed.

The man took two steps into the alley and stopped.

That was when Rin stepped out from the overhang and spoke.

"You waited too long to be subtle," she said.

The man turned. The blankness in his face did not change. If anything, it sharpened.

"You were supposed to be easier to miss," he said.

"You should've tried harder."

Yuki came up from the other side, silent for once, every inch of her alert. The man looked from one to the other and seemed, for the first time, to understand that he had walked into a room with more than one person in it.

That was the first mistake.

Rin moved first. She didn't waste a breath. She took the first step that mattered and drove forward, low and fast, and the man turned to meet her — but he was slower than he looked, slower than the people who had never had to be seen. She caught the edge of his sleeve, twisted, and sent him off-balance. Yuki darted in from the other side, a flash of motion and motion and motion again, all instinct and speed, and the man made a sound of frustration rather than fear.

He was not a fighter the way Kyoya's people were. He was a cleaner. A handler. A professional who had decided that patience was a weapon, and now had discovered the cost of being too proud to use it properly.

He tried to run.

That was the second mistake.

Rin took three steps and caught him by the back of the coat. Yuki grabbed the opposite arm and together they dragged him into the light. He kicked, hard, and clipped Rin's leg, but the angle was wrong and the blow was weak. She slammed him against the wall and kept him there. Yuki fixed him with a glare sharp enough to strip paint.

"All right," Yuki said, breathless. "Who are you, exactly?"

The man hesitated.

That was the third mistake.

Rin leaned in, close enough to see the tiny pulse ticking in his throat. "You want to know what I don't like? People who stop being subtle when they think it's safe. Start telling us what you want."

The man looked at them, looked at the alley, looked past them at the street beyond, and then something in his face changed. It went from blank to practical.

"I was told to watch her," he said. "That's all."

"Whose orders?"

He swallowed. "I don't know. I only know the name."

Yuki and Rin exchanged a look.

"Say it."

The man breathed out. "The House."

The word landed in the alley like a dropped weight.

Rin stared at him.

Yuki's ears flattened. "That's not a person."

The man shook his head once, though it was not clear whether he meant no, or not in the way we think. "That's the only name I was given."

Rin looked at the alley, the station entrance, the road beyond it, and the city surrounding it all in one sharp clean sweep. The House. The name meant nothing to her, and that was the problem. It meant enough to someone. Enough to send a man to watch a girl on a campus and then be disappointed when she noticed.

"You can go now," she said.

The man blinked. "What?"

"You heard me. You're done." Rin stepped back and let him straighten. "But if you come back, you won't get the courtesy of a warning."

The man hesitated, looked once at Yuki's expression, once at Rin's face, and then nodded. He turned and walked away.

They stood in the alley a moment longer, breathing hard, until the city noise went back to normal around them.

Yuki let out a long breath. "Okay. That was not how I thought my night would go."

Rin looked at the empty street. "No. But it was useful."

Yuki looked at her. "You always say that like a threat."

Rin turned and started walking. "You should be glad it's still useful."

Yuki grinned despite herself and fell into step beside her. "I think I like being on the edges of your dramatic little plans."

"You don't have a choice."

"That's a very comforting thing to hear."
