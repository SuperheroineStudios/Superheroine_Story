Chapter Thirty-One — What's Left Standing

The police who came were the quiet kind — the small handful who answered calls like this and knew how to answer them, arriving after the sirens as the dawn went from gray to gold.

Ryo did the talking, of course. Even flat on a stretcher, ribs taped, one eye swelling shut, he had exactly the right words for the officers leaning over him — calm, spare, a version of the night that fit into the shapes they needed it to fit. He always knew what to say. His own kind of genesis, the mind that stayed three steps ahead even wrecked and bleeding on a backboard.

They loaded him into an ambulance, and Mei into another — still unconscious, but breathing steady, her vitals fine, just spent from a crash she'd caused on purpose to save all their lives. Rin watched the doors close on both of them and made herself a quiet promise to be there when they opened again.

Faith was up, moving stiff but upright, walking the lead officer through the situation in that low even voice that made people believe her. Jeanne was gone — she'd melted off into the waking city before anyone thought to ask who the woman with green in her hair had been, keeping her freedom and her secrets, a wildcard slipping back into the deck.

A paramedic caught Rin's arm, frowning at the state of her — the closed slash on her shoulder, the bruises, the blood that wasn't all hers. "Miss, you two are in pretty rough shape, you should really let us—"

"They're with me," Faith said, not even looking over. "Special orders. They'll be fine." And something in the way she said it made the paramedic nod and step back, no further questions, and Rin filed away one more piece of the mystery of what exactly Faith Kiryu was.

A few feet off, Yuki had recovered enough to stand and was submitting, badly, to being checked over by another officer — who kept glancing, a beat too long, at her ears. And her tail. Yuki's smile went tight and her ears flattened, and Rin caught her eye and gave a small shake of the head, don't, and Yuki swallowed whatever she'd been about to say and tucked the tail out of sight, and let it go.

They took the thugs away in a slow procession of unconscious bodies. And they took Kyoya — the man Rin had chosen not to kill, too broken to lift his head, loaded into a car with his hands bound and his empire in ruins around him. Alive. Caged. For a man like that, Rin thought, watching the door shut on him, it might be the worse of the two mercies.

The compound was secured. The lot emptied. And in the end, under Faith's word, a pair of officers offered to run the girls home.



Rin and Yuki took the back seat, the city sliding past golden and calm in the after-light.

Somewhere in the quiet, Yuki's hand found hers. Didn't say anything about it. Just held on, the way she had on a long walk home weeks ago, and Rin let her, and looked out the window at a city that had, for one night, tried very hard to kill all of them and failed.

She turned it over as they drove. The fight. Amanda — that impossible, immovable strength, a wall none of them could break and had barely, barely survived. Mei behind a steering wheel with her jaw set. Ryo grinning down a bloodied road with a phone in his hand. All of them, each doing the one thing only they could do, at the one moment it mattered. They'd lived because of that. Not because any one of them was strong enough. Because together they were something Amanda hadn't accounted for.

And underneath the exhaustion, Rin let herself feel one small, sharp, worthy flicker of satisfaction — because somewhere out there a certain blonde devil had arranged all of this to watch Rin become a killer, to shape her into a weapon for whatever game she was playing.

And Rin hadn't done it. She'd won, and stayed herself, and denied the devil the one thing she'd wanted.

You didn't get your way, she thought at the golden morning, smug. Not this time.



A couple of weeks passed.

Rin took her classes as normally as her life allowed, which was to say not very, but she got through them. Liliana lectured and took attendance and was, still, a perfectly pleasant professor — the quiet devil, biding whatever she was biding, and Rin the only soul in the building who knew what sat behind those pleasant eyes. She kept it that way. Some truths still kept people safer unspoken.

Vomi asked after Mei every single day. How is she. Is she resting. Tell her I expect her back at full strength. Concern, warm on the surface — and under it, maybe, the particular interest of a scientist who'd once scanned a room and wondered about a girl who kept turning up at the center of things. Rin noted it, and said nothing, and added it to the small pile of questions she was saving for later.

The city, for its part, exhaled. With Kyoya caged and his ring shattered, the crime that had been swallowing whole neighborhoods simply… receded. The green-marked crews scattered or vanished. The nights got quieter. The fear that had held the streets loosened its grip, block by block, and for the first time since Rin had come to this city, the dark felt less like something waiting.

And the menacing purple titan did not reappear.

Rin was certain she would. Amanda hadn't been beaten — she'd left, wounded and interested, filing Rin away for a day of her choosing. That reckoning was still out there, somewhere down the line. But it wasn't today. Today the city was quiet, and that was enough.



At the end of the school day, Rin found Yuki waiting outside, and they walked the courtyard together the way they did now, trading the small nothings of a normal week — training, mostly; Rin chasing her speed, Yuki her endurance, both of them building quietly on the foundations they'd found. Nothing had tried to kill either of them in fourteen whole days. It was, Yuki declared, boring in the best possible way.

Then Rin looked toward the gate, and stopped.

Because there, strolling onto campus with his hands in his pockets and a coffee can in one and his tie a rumor, entirely recovered, was Ryo — and the moment his eyes found Rin across the courtyard, the flat unbothered mask cracked into something warmer than he'd ever admit to, the quiet glad look of a man seeing his favorite person alive and whole and waiting.

And a step behind him, bouncing, arms already flung wide, cardigan sleeves flapping, fully mended and twice as loud about it, came Mei.

"They're back—" Yuki was already gone, tearing across the courtyard toward the two of them, ears up, whooping, and Ryo braced himself half a second too late for the collision.

Rin stayed where she was a moment longer, and just looked at them — Yuki barreling into Mei, Mei laughing, Ryo enduring it all with that almost-smile, the whole battered, impossible, precious lot of them, healed and here and hers.

A month ago she'd had one person and an apartment she never decorated and a body that only felt like hers in a fight.

Now she had this.

Rin Aizawa smiled — a real one, unguarded, all the way up to her pink eyes — and went to join her friends.



End of Act One.
