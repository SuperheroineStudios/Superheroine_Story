Chapter Thirty — What It Takes

Three of them against Amanda, and Amanda was having the time of her life.

Rin felt the truth of it settle cold in her gut as the woman rolled her shoulders and grinned: there was no angle here. No boxing her in, no baiting her, no helpless-plea trick, no clever thing Rin could pull to steal the win. She'd beaten Kyoya with cunning because Kyoya had a flaw she could reach. Amanda had none she could see. This was just a wall, and walls didn't fall for tricks.

She spared a glance back — Yuki and Mei pressed together, frozen, Yuki too hurt to fight and Mei with nothing to fight with, both of them wide-eyed and small against the dawn. Whatever happened next was on Rin, and on the two who moved up to flank her: Faith on her left, Jeanne on her right, the two strongest fighters, both grim.

"Whatever you've got," Rin murmured. "All of it."

Amanda strode in.

She swiped — one arm, casual, a blow that would've taken any of their heads off — and the three scattered out of its path. And then they moved, together, the way they'd learned the hard way it took. Faith flashed in from one side and buried a kick into Amanda's ribs; the stagger tipped her straight into a hammering body chop from Jeanne on the other; and Rin's speed genesis fired, the blur carrying her under Amanda's guard into a rising uppercut that snapped the giant's head back.

Amanda rocked backward. Her chin up.

For one heartbeat, they'd hurt her.

Then she slowly lowered her head, and the smirk hadn't moved an inch.

Her push kick caught Rin square and launched her back across the lot. Faith lunged to follow up and never landed it — Amanda's backhand met her mid-motion and sent her spinning away. Jeanne came in low and Amanda simply caught her by the collar, no theatrics, and slammed her flat into the asphalt hard enough to knock the wind clean out of her.

Rin was already back up, throwing herself between Amanda and the fallen viper. She slipped the next swing and cracked a blow across Amanda's face — the head turned — and then turned back, lilac eyes finding her again, unbothered, and a fist came around that would have ended Rin where she stood—

Jeanne's leg shot out from the ground and kicked Amanda's knee out from under the strike. The blow whiffed past Rin's ear. And Rin planted and put her strength genesis into a kick that caught Amanda flush in the jaw.

Faith was up again — a speed-blurred rush, a kick to the body that staggered Amanda a full step back.

The three of them fell back together, chests heaving, spent. They'd landed everything. They'd hit her with the best combined assault Rin could imagine, three genesis fighters at full tilt.

And Amanda straightened up, cracked her neck, and her eyes flared a deep, furious purple.

Now she was taking it seriously.

She advanced, and it was a different thing entirely. Jeanne struck first and Amanda deflected it like brushing off a fly, caught the arm, and threw her — the viper sailing the length of the lot to slam into a wall and slide down it, slumped and still. Faith came next and got a boot to the gut that folded her double and dumped her sprawling across the ground, gasping, done.

And then it was just Rin.

Amanda's hand closed around her collar and lifted her off her feet, that furious purple gaze holding hers, the other fist drawing back to strike a blow Rin knew, with cold certainty, she would not get up from.

Behind her, Yuki screamed her name, helpless.

"So sad," Amanda said, almost gentle, "that it has to end this way, little girl—"

SKRRRRRT.

RRRRRRMMMM.

An engine, gunning. Amanda's head snapped toward the sound — and there was no time for her to solve it, because the jeep was already screaming across the lot straight at her, headlights blazing in the gray dawn, Mei white-knuckled and terrified behind the wheel with her foot flat to the floor.

For one instant, Amanda's attention split entirely to the oncoming car.

One instant was all Rin needed.

She hauled every last scrap of everything she had into one kick — hanging there in Amanda's grip, twisting, driving her heel up into the woman's face with a strength born of pure desperation — and Amanda's head rocked, the grip loosened, and Rin dropped free—

BOOM.

The jeep hit Amanda full-on.

The impact was enormous. Even durable as she was — even built like something that shrugged off buildings — Amanda was launched off her feet, hurled down the side of the road to crash into a storefront wall hard enough to crater it. Rin hit the ground and rolled clear, staring, and thought, with grim satisfaction: that had to hurt. Even her. That had to hurt.

The jeep shuddered to a stop, steam hissing from its crumpled hood.

Mei was slumped unconscious over the wheel.

"Mei—!" Yuki was already scrambling toward the car on her bad leg, and Rin's heart lurched, but she could see it — Mei was breathing, just out cold from the crash, having spent the only weapon she had and it had been enough. Rin would thank her properly later. For all of it. For being brave with nothing but a steering wheel and a refusal to watch her friends die.

Down the road, in the ruin of the storefront, Amanda stirred. Dragged an arm under herself. Began, impossibly, to rise.

And then Rin heard it — cutting through the ringing dawn, faint and getting louder.

Sirens.

She spun, scanning. Who? Jeanne was slumped. Faith was down and unmoving. Yuki and Mei were at the jeep. There was no one left standing who could have—

Ryo.

Down the road, far from the fight where Amanda had kicked him, he'd rolled onto his side. Broken, battered, still barely able to move — but there was a phone in his hand, screen lit, and a small, satisfied, exhausted grin on his face as the sirens swelled toward them. He'd been down the whole time. He'd never been out. The boy who thought three moves ahead of everyone had spent his one card at exactly the right moment: not a punch, but a call.

Amanda heard the sirens too.

She got the rest of the way to her feet, swaying, one hand pressed to her ribs where the jeep had caved something in, and Rin watched the calculation move behind those lilac eyes — the same cold business-first arithmetic that ran everything she did. Finishing them was possible. But finishing them now, wounded, with police closing in on an open lot full of bodies, meant risking capture, and Amanda did not risk capture for a grudge. The money was already gone. There was nothing here worth a cell.

She straightened, and fixed Rin with one long, final look — not hatred. Something colder and more patient. Interest. The look of a woman filing a name away for later.

Then she turned and left, moving fast even hurt, abandoning the fallen compound and Kyoya and every last one of her men, vanishing into the tangle of the waking city as her whole empire lay broken behind her.

Rin stayed on her feet until she was sure — until the purple was gone and the sirens were close and the monster wasn't coming back.

Then her legs gave out, and she dropped to her knees in the cracked and bloodied lot, gasping, the adrenaline draining out of her all at once and leaving her hollow and shaking and alive.

We did it, she thought, looking around at them — Jeanne stirring against her wall, Faith pushing weakly up onto an elbow, Yuki cradling unconscious Mei in the crumpled jeep, Ryo grinning his exhausted grin down the road with a phone still in his hand.

Battered. Every single one of them. But breathing.

We survived.
