Chapter Fifteen — Three Roads

The jeep moved through the city like it had nowhere in particular to be.

That was the first thing Rin noticed, sitting cuffed in the passenger seat with the buildings sliding past: the Viper wasn't hurrying. She drove smoothly, one hand loose on the wheel, taking unhurried turns, letting lights go yellow-to-red rather than gun through them. A woman delivering a package she was in no rush to hand over.

And there was a look on her face Rin couldn't quite parse. She'd catalogued a lot of faces in a lot of bad rooms — the ones who enjoyed it, the ones who'd gone hollow, the ones playing tough over terror. This wasn't any of those. It was something quieter and stranger. Not the flat calm of someone about to kill. More like someone driving a person to their execution and finding, to her own faint annoyance, that she didn't entirely have the stomach for it.

"I'm going to die," Rin said. Not a question. Just laying it on the dash between them, the plain arithmetic of it. Kyoya had promised to keep a blade sharp for her specifically. She could add.

The Viper didn't flinch from it. "Yeah," she said, eyes on the road. "You probably will." A beat. "Because you're not going to take the other option, are you. He'll offer you one — work for him, kneel, be useful. And you're going to spit it back in his face, because you're the defiant type. It's written all over you." She almost smiled. "I've delivered enough people to know which ones walk back out. You're not one of them."

Rin said nothing.

"For what it's worth," the Viper went on, and there was that unreadable thing again under her voice, "he took a specific interest in you. That's rare. He collects talent, sure, but this was different. He wanted you, by name, by face." Her green eyes flicked over, assessing. "You stand out — I'll grant you that, the whole room curled up and you didn't. But I've been turning it over the whole drive and I still can't work out what he actually wants from a scrappy little student who moonlights folding freelancers. What makes you worth all this."

Neither can I, Rin thought. And then, sharper, following the thread all the way down: neither can you — so someone told him.

Because that was the piece that didn't fit, and it had been nagging at her since the sidewalk. Kyoya hadn't stumbled onto her. The Viper had rolled up to that exact café, at that exact hour, on the one free afternoon Rin had. He said the eyes would be right. He'd known she'd be there. And Rin had told no one where she was going today. No one except Mei — and Mei had planned the whole thing, Mei who she'd known barely a week but knew, bone-deep, would sooner cut off her own hand than breathe a word to people like this. Mei wasn't it. Mei couldn't be it.

So then who?

Someone was standing behind Kyoya, feeding him. Someone with eyes on her, close enough to know a girl's plans on a quiet Sunday. The thought raised the hair on her arms — and, unbidden, a memory surfaced and hung there: a red-eyed woman at a gate, warning her to protect Mei today, as though she'd already read the last page. Rin turned it over, felt the edge of something enormous and cold, and couldn't quite close her hand around it.

The jeep slowed. Turned. Rolled through a chain gate into the shadow of a long, windowless building at the industrial edge of the city.

They'd arrived.
