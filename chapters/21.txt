Chapter Twenty-One — What We Are

They took over a corner of the dorm common room that evening, the four of them, and had the strangest conversation of Rin's life.

"So let me get this straight." Yuki was upside down in an armchair, legs hooked over the back, ears twitching as she thought. "This whole time — the ears, the hearing, the whole cat situation — that's not just, like, how I was born weird. That's a genesis ability?" She frowned at the ceiling. "I never once thought of it like that. I thought I was just… me. A person who's good at hearing things and bad at hiding behind dumpsters."

"You are bad at hiding behind dumpsters," Rin said. "But now we know why they always found you. You've been lit up like a bonfire the whole time to anyone who can read it."

"That's so unfair."

"It's your ears." Rin turned to the other end of the couch. "And you. You're the brains. You always have been." She met Ryo's eyes. "You could have straight-A's in your sleep if you gave a single damn — and you don't, and you still see three moves ahead of everyone in the room. That's not just smart, Ryo. I think that's the gene. Yours does the thinking."

"He doesn't want good grades," Yuki said sweetly, flipping upright. "He just wants you. Right, Ryo? Big brain, one priority."

Ryo didn't rise to it. He looked, very deliberately, at the middle distance, the way he did when a thing landed too close to true. But under the deflection something else was working — a small, uncharacteristic frown. He did know he was smart. He'd always known, in a quiet private way, that his mind ran faster than it had any business running, that he saw the shape of things before other people got their bearings. What was needling him was that Rin had gotten here first. She'd assembled the whole picture — the gene, the categories, who had what — before he had. He'd only half-caught the edge of it himself, once he'd heard what Faith could do.

"…I was getting there," he muttered. "After the Faith thing. You just beat me to saying it."

"First time for everything," Rin said, and the corner of his mouth twitched despite him.

"Wait, wait." Mei had been curled quiet in the corner, absorbing all of it with enormous eyes, and now she sat up. "What about me? Do I — could I have one? Is there, like, a me one?"

"Ooh." Yuki rounded on her, delighted. "Mei's power. Let's see. Aggressive note-taking? Sticky-note telekinesis? The ability to make anyone tell you their whole life story in four seconds?"

"Those are real skills," Mei protested.

"The power of snacks?"

"You like my snacks—"

"I love your snacks, that's the point, it's clearly mind control—"

Mei huffed, going pink, and threw a cushion, and Yuki caught it laughing, and for a moment it was just four teenagers in a common room being ridiculous, and Rin let herself sit in the warmth of it.

But underneath, quietly, another thought was turning.

I could be stronger. Vomi had said it plainly — the abilities were cultivated, pushed past the ceiling through stress and repetition and the edge of breaking. Rin had been building hers by accident her whole life. What could she build on purpose? She'd survived a fall that should've killed her; she could feel the endurance in her like a foundation. What sat on top of it, waiting? She flexed a hand and thought about limits and how you found them, and realized, with a familiar cold thrill, that she wanted a fight. Not for the money. To test it. To push and see what pushed back.

There were no contracts on her phone. For the first time in a long time, she wished there were.

The next one couldn't come soon enough.
