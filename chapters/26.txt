Chapter Twenty-Six — One More Dance

Rin stood in the dark grass and did the math, and the math was ugly.

Yuki was behind Kyoya, curled around a wound that was bleeding too much, too fast. There was no boxing him in this time — no Yuki cutting angles, no wounded Faith holding a lane, no crew. Just her, and him, and a park with too much open space in it. Two options: fight, or oblige.

She was done obliging.

"You know how this could go instead." Kyoya reached into his coat and drew out a second blade — he had a collection, it seemed, a whole sheath of them against his ribs — and held it out to her, hilt first, almost tender. "Take it. Say yes. Stand with me and I'll give you the whole city, and we'll never have to do the hurting-each-other part again." His eyes shone. "You and me. Doesn't that sound good?"

He genuinely didn't understand. That was the strangest part. He'd built a whole fantasy of her — the fire, the fellow monster, the girl who'd leap at a chance to fight the woman who'd thrown her out a window — and he couldn't see how completely he'd gotten her wrong.

Because a month ago, maybe. A month ago she'd been a girl who only felt alive on the edge, who chased danger because it was the one thing that made the static go quiet. But she wasn't that girl anymore. She'd found something — a roommate who patched her up, a cat who held her hand, a boy who'd drive through a wall for her, a woman in red on a golden hill. She didn't crave the violence the way she used to. She had somewhere warm to stand now, and she wasn't trading it for a knife and a madman's kingdom.

"No," Rin said.

Kyoya's face fell, and it was almost pitiable — genuine, wounded disappointment. "No? But — the fight, the — Amanda threw you out a window, Rin, don't you want to make her pay? Don't you want to be strong? I'm handing you—"

"I don't care about your war." Rin's voice was flat and cold and certain. "I don't care about your boss, or your city, or your obsession. I care about the girl bleeding behind you." She dropped into her stance, weight settling, everything in her narrowing to one single purpose. "And nothing is getting between me and her."

Kyoya opened his mouth to argue.

Rin attacked first.

He was fast. She'd known he would be, and it was still worse than she'd braced for — he read her opening rush and simply wasn't where it landed, sliding out of her combination with that gleeful, slippery ease, laughing again, dancing back into the open where he had all the room in the world. Out here, uncornered, he was a nightmare. No walls, no allies, nothing to pin him against.

But she'd been training. And when he got cocky — when he drifted in close to taunt, too confident, too sure she couldn't touch him — Rin planted and drove a hook into his gut with everything the new strength gave her.

It landed. The breath punched out of him, the grin flickered, and for a second real surprise crossed his face.

Then the grin died entirely.

"…Alright," Kyoya said softly, straightening, rolling his shoulders. "If you want to die that badly. I was going to be gentle." His eyes flared — a vivid, sourceless green, the same wrongness she'd seen in Faith, in the Viper — and everything changed.

He came at her at a speed that made his earlier dancing look lazy, the giant blade sweeping around in an arc she felt more than saw — and Rin threw everything she'd built into her legs and moved, her own trained speed hauling her out of the path by inches. The blade cut nothing but air where she'd been — and the force of it, the sheer power riding that speed, sheared clean through a park bench ten feet behind her, dropping both halves to the grass.

Speed and strength both, Rin understood, cold. His genesis is both at once. That was why he could dance like the Viper and cut like a truck. He wasn't one thing. He was two, stacked.

She wasn't fast enough to answer that.

His hand shot out and closed in her hair, yanking her off her rhythm — and a knee slammed into her gut, folding her, then a second, driving the air out of her in a rush. She got a hand up, broke his grip with a desperate strike, tore free — and he batted her away with the heavy hilt of the blade, a casual blow that sent her stumbling back, winded, spots swimming.

She didn't get her guard up in time for the next one.

He dashed in and the blade came down in a real slash, and Rin couldn't clear it — it opened a hot line across her back shoulder, deep, and the force of it spun her off her feet and slammed her down into the grass, facedown, the world going white and roaring.

"Heh." Kyoya's voice, above her, delighted again. "You know, you look good like that."

Rin pushed against the ground. Her arms shook. Her shoulder screamed. She got halfway up—

—and Kyoya's hand fisted the back of her collar and hauled her the rest of the way, lifting her to her feet like she weighed nothing, holding her steady when her legs wouldn't. He tilted his head, studying her dazed, bloodied face with something that was almost affection.

"Beautiful, feisty," he said. He pressed the tip of the blade against her stomach — gently, almost apologetic, just enough to dimple the fabric. "This is your last chance, you know. Truly. Say the word — say you'll come with me — and the knife goes away and none of this happened. Or don't, and I finish it, and I'll be so, so sad about it." His eyes searched hers. "Just say the word, Rin Aizawa."

Rin hung in his grip, and the anger burned through the daze — cold and clarifying.

The devil woman is watching this. She knew it in her bones — Liliana, somewhere in the dark, enjoying the show she'd arranged. Mei will cry if I die here. Ryo will blame himself for the rest of his life for not coming. And behind Kyoya, Yuki was dragging herself up on one elbow, bleeding, reaching toward her with a hand that couldn't reach, her mouth shaping Rin's name.

Rin had one attempt left in her. Maybe. One.

Rin let her body go soft.

She let the fight drain out of her stance, let her eyes go wide and wet and helpless, let her face fall into something small and pleading and afraid — the exact opposite of everything Kyoya had ever seen from her, and therefore the one thing that could stop him cold.

Kyoya's breath caught. The blade wavered.

"…Okay," Rin whispered, broken, and slowly — trembling — she lifted a hand and laid it flat against his chest. He went utterly still. She slid it upward, slow, deliberate, until her fingers rested at the side of his neck, and she looked up at him through her lashes with everything she had, and gave him the exact words she knew he'd been starving to hear. "Okay. You win. I'll come with you. I'll stand with you, I'll fight for you… I'll love you, Kyoya. Just — put it down. Please."

Every bone in Kyoya's body locked.

His eyes went huge and shining and disbelieving and thrilled, the obsession he'd fed for weeks finally, impossibly, blooming into a yes. His grip loosened. The blade drifted away from her stomach as his whole focus collapsed inward onto the miracle of her hand on his neck and her promise in his ears. He'd done it. He'd won. She was—

Rin's hand clamped around his wrist. Her eyes flared blue.

She hauled on it with every last scrap of genesis strength she had left, twisting, reversing, driving his own arm and his own blade back around — and buried the knife to the hilt in Kyoya's gut.

Yuki gasped in shock.

Kyoya screamed. He staggered backward off her, hands scrabbling at the blade, and — in a panic — wrenched it out of himself, which only made it worse, blood coming fast and dark, the knife clattering from his fingers to the grass.

All's fair in love and war.

Rin dropped to her knees, spent, the last of her strength gone with the twist.

Kyoya reeled, clutching his stomach, staring at the ruin of himself and then at her with an expression of pure, betrayed astonishment. "You—" He choked on it. "You — how dare you. How — I gave you — you can't, you can't do this to me, I — after everything I—" His voice cracked into a wounded howl.

And then the animal under the obsession took over, the coward reasserting itself the way it always did the moment the deck turned. He clamped both hands over his gut, turned, and ran — a lurching, stumbling sprint into the dark, leaving his blade and his fantasy and a trail of his own blood behind him.

Rin knelt in the grass, breathing in ragged pulls, and felt the adrenaline surge back through her — that old terrible gift, the body answering an emergency with more than it should have. She dragged herself up, swaying, and turned to Yuki.

"Kitten—"

"I'm okay." Yuki's voice was thin but there, and she waved Rin off with a bloody hand, propped against the ground. "It's — it hurts like hell but it's not — I'll live." She grimaced, then jerked her chin at the dark where Kyoya had vanished. "Go. Go get him. He's hurt, he's slow, this is the chance, Rin — if he gets away he just comes back—"

Rin looked toward the dark. Kyoya was out there, bleeding, staggering, catchable — maybe. On a good day, at full strength, she could run him down.

This was not a good day. Her shoulder was open, her legs were water, and she'd spent everything on that one twist. She'd never catch him like this. She took one step and nearly went down.

SCREECH.

Headlights swung across the grass. A sleek shape braked hard at the park's edge, a door already swinging open — and the Viper leaned across from the driver's seat, green eyes fixed on Rin, and said the last thing Rin expected to hear.

"Get in." Jeanne's voice was sharp, urgent, and — impossibly — on her side. "If you want him, we go now. I'll explain on the way."

Rin looked back at Yuki.

"Go," Yuki insisted. "I've got me. Go."

One more heartbeat of hesitation — making sure, truly sure, that Yuki was steady — and then Rin turned and threw herself into the passenger seat, and the Viper hit the gas before the door was even shut, and they tore off into the dark after a bleeding man and every answer he was running from.
