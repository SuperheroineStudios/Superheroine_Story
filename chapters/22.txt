Chapter Twenty-Two — Enough

Rin read the fight before it started, the way she always did.

Three goons down and out. Faith on one knee behind her, bleeding from three long lines Kyoya had opened across her side and shoulder, still upright but pale, her endurance the only thing keeping her in the picture. And Kyoya himself, a few paces off, rolling the tension out of his neck, that giant blade loose and forgotten in one hand, the manic grin never leaving his face.

He was reading her right back. She could see it — the flat bright eyes flicking over her stance, her guard, the space between them, doing the same math she was. Except his attention kept sliding off everything else and landing on her. Not Yuki. Not the wounded woman on the ground. Her. Like the rest of the alley was set dressing and Rin was the only real thing in it.

"You came," he breathed, delighted. "You actually came. For her." He tipped the blade toward Faith without looking away from Rin. "You have no idea how happy that makes me. I've been chasing you across a whole city and here you are, walking into me. It's like you knew I needed the pick-me-up."

"Yuki," Rin said quietly, not taking her eyes off him.

Yuki rolled her shoulders, ears pinned forward, tail lashing. "He talks a lot for a guy about to get his teeth kicked in."

"I love her," Kyoya said. "Can't wait to gut the both of you."

He moved.

And Rin's read went sideways immediately, because he did not move like anything she'd fought. The goons had been power and swings, readable arcs she could slip in her sleep. Kyoya came in wrong — a burst of speed that ate the distance, then a stutter, a fake, a laugh, and he was already inside her guard, close and personal, one hand snapping out to fist her collar while his knee drove up toward her ribs. Not the blade. The blade was an afterthought hanging in his off-hand. It was his body that was the weapon, all grabs and joints and that horrible glee.

Rin twisted, took the knee on a turned hip instead of her floating ribs — it still cracked hard enough to steal her breath — and broke his grip with a downward strike, giving ground.
