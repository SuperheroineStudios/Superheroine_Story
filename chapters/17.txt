Chapter Seventeen — Let Them Pass

Jeanne watched the girl go through the window and felt something she didn't have a good name for.

It wasn't shock. She'd known, from the moment Amanda's hand closed on that collar, that it was going to end badly — the boss lady didn't lift people to talk to them. And it wasn't guilt, exactly; Jeanne had done a great deal in her life and made her peace with most of it. It was closer to the small, worn-down sorrow of watching a waste happen. The kid had fire. Real fire, the kind you couldn't teach, the kind Jeanne had seen maybe three times in all her years in the dark. And Amanda had flicked her across a room like crumbs off a table because she'd been bored.

Shame, Jeanne thought, as the glass finished falling. That's what it was. A shame.

Across the room, Kyoya had gone very still.

He was doing an admirable job of hiding it, she'd give him that — the manic grin plastered back on, the easy posture — but Jeanne had spent enough time reading dangerous men to see the fury vibrating underneath. He hadn't wanted that. He'd wanted his prize, his recruit, his little obsession kneeling at his side. Amanda had just thrown it out a window in front of him, and there was not one single thing Kyoya could do about it, because the alternative to swallowing his anger was saying something to Amanda, and saying something to Amanda got you folded in half and mailed home in a cooler. She'd squash any of them the instant it stopped being inconvenient. Kyoya knew it. Jeanne knew it. Everyone in the building knew it, which was exactly why Amanda ran the whole rotten thing.

Amanda was already turning away, done, bored again, climbing back toward wherever she'd come from as though she'd swatted a fly.

The moment she was gone, Kyoya's eyes cut to Jeanne. Just a look. But she'd worked for him long enough to translate it, and under the surface meaning — go check, clean it up — there was a second one he'd never say out loud with Amanda's footsteps still fading on the stairs. A flicker of something desperate. Go see. As if a man could hope a body off a pavement.

Jeanne sighed, internally, and headed for the stairs.

Scraping a corpse off the concrete, she thought. Really living the dream today.
