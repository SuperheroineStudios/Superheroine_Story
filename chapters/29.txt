Chapter Twenty-Nine — The Purple Threat

CRASH.

The sound tore across the lot, and everyone turned toward it at once — Rin, Faith, Jeanne by the compound; Mei and Yuki mid-sprint across the open ground. Rin's eyes went past the two of them, past the running girls who were now looking back over their shoulders too, to a spot down the road where the gray dawn had just been broken.

Ryo.

And something towering over him.

She'd leapt — that was the crash, Rin understood in a cold rush, the impact of a seven-foot woman dropping three stories to land directly in front of him, cracking the asphalt where she touched down. Amanda. She stood over Ryo now, unhurried, looking down at him with nothing but amusement and a slow, sick, sadistic smile.

"Ryo—" Rin was already moving. So was Faith. So was Jeanne. And all three of them knew, in the same breath, that they were too far. A hundred feet of open ground. No time.

Amanda reached down and picked Ryo up by his collar, lifting him off his feet one-handed, never once taking her eyes off his face. He grabbed at her wrist, weak, straining, and couldn't move it an inch — a slacker who'd never trained a day of the gift he carried, hanging helpless in the grip of the strongest thing in the city.

"Good-bye, pretty boy," Amanda said.

She drove her boot into his chest.

The kick sent Ryo flying — down the road, limbs loose, a rag doll hurled the length of the block — and he hit the ground hard and rolled and tumbled and came to rest facedown, unmoving.

"RYO!" Yuki's scream cracked the morning in half.

Something in Rin's chest tore.

He hit and he didn't move. Didn't push up, didn't groan, didn't do the thing Ryo always did which was be fine, be dry, be there. He just lay in the road, facedown and still, and there was too much distance and a monster between them, and no way to reach him, no way to even know if he was breathing.

Amanda turned back toward the rest of them, boots scuffing the cracked lot, between the fallen boy and everyone who might run to him. Her eyes flared a deep, wrong purple over that spreading smile. You can't get to him, the whole posture said. Not past me.

Rin planted herself in front of Mei and Yuki and looked up at the woman, and the anger in her was a cold, total, killing thing.

She knew. She knew this was exactly what the devil wanted — Liliana out there somewhere, delighted, having lost her weapon on Kyoya only to have Rin's rage handed right back to her by someone bigger. Rin knew it was bait.

She didn't care. For what Amanda had just done to Ryo, she would kill this woman or die trying.

Amanda, for her part, was almost enjoying herself.

"You know," Amanda called across the lot, her voice easy and amused, "this has been more entertaining than my week usually runs." She began a slow, unhurried pace. "Kyoya — throwing an entire operation into the fire over a schoolgirl crush. Pathetic." A low chuckle. "But a fascinating kind of pathetic, I'll give him that."

Her lilac gaze settled on Rin, and something in it shifted — a weighing, appraising interest. "And then there's you. The little thing I flicked out a third-story window without a second thought. And here you are — not just breathing, but back, taking his whole crew apart like they were made of wet paper." The smile sharpened. "That's rare. I don't say it often, so enjoy it: I'm almost impressed."

The smile cooled a degree. "It's a shame about the money, though. With Kyoya bleeding out in a doorway, this month's number doesn't come in — which means I have to go and earn it. Rebuild. Lean on people. Grind." Distaste flickered across her face. "I hate working. That, more than anything, is what brought me down off that roof."

But there was an upside — and she wanted the girl to hear it.

"You've done me a favor, in a way," she mused, her voice carrying easy across the lot. "All my little problems, lining themselves up in one place at dawn. So considerate." Her lilac gaze swept them — the wounded girls, the woman in red, the traitor with green in her hair. "The Crimson Lotus, who's been such a persistent little splinter. My own Viper, who seems to have forgotten which hand feeds her. And whatever's left of Kyoya's obsession." The smile widened. "I do love it when the weeding does itself."

Rin's mind raced under the fury, cold and fast. We can't win this. Not her alone — that was obvious. And maybe not even with Faith and Jeanne at her back, the two strongest fighters she'd ever stood beside, who had moved up behind her now and were both, she could feel it, doing the same grim arithmetic and hating the answer.

Amanda didn't give them time to finish the math.

She attacked first.

It was almost lazy — a backhand swipe of one enormous arm — and it caught Rin across the guard and swatted her off her feet like she weighed nothing, sending her skidding across the asphalt.

"Rin—!" Mei's cry.

Rin caught herself on her hands, ears ringing. And through the ringing she saw Faith and Jeanne move — a coordinated counter, the veteran and the viper striking from two angles at once, fast and clean and good—

Amanda caught them both by the throat. One hand each. Plucked them out of the air mid-strike like she was catching flies, and lifted, their feet leaving the ground, both of them clawing at fingers that didn't budge — and Rin saw her arms tense to finish it, to crush two of the most dangerous people in the city in the space of a breath—

No.

Rin's speed genesis fired.

She didn't decide it — her body simply went, every jogged mile and every hilltop lesson and everything she'd bled to build detonating at once, and she crossed the distance in a blur that skipped frames and slammed into Amanda shoulder-first with a strength she hadn't known was in her.

It worked. Amanda was launched sideways, staggering, her grip tearing loose — and Faith and Jeanne dropped, gasping, to their knees.

For half a second Rin felt something like hope.

Then Amanda recovered — and she was fast too. Of course she was. The purple flared in her eyes and she dashed back across the gap in a blur of her own that matched Rin's stride for stride, and drove a shoulder into her that hit like a truck, knocking Rin clean off her feet. Rin hit the ground and looked up—

Amanda was already in the air above her, both boots coming down to crush her flat.

Rin rolled. The boots cratered the asphalt where she'd been, a spiderweb of cracks blooming out from the impact, and she scrambled up and back, putting distance between herself and the giant woman, regrouping with Faith and Jeanne who'd hauled themselves upright.

Amanda tilted her head back and watched the three of them cluster, in no hurry at all, rolling her shoulders like she'd barely warmed up.

"That could've gone worse," Jeanne rasped, one hand at her bruised throat, settling back into a stance.

"Mm." Faith cracked her neck, wincing, and raised her guard. "Marginally."

Rin said nothing. She stood between her friends and the monster, breathing hard, the speed still humming in her legs — and looked, past Amanda, down the long road to where Ryo lay facedown in the dawn and still, still, had not moved.
