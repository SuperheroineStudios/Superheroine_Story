Chapter Nineteen — Two Professors

Morning came gray and slow, and Rin woke into an ache that had settled from screaming into a dull, bone-deep hum.

Yuki was a silver lump on the floor between the beds, tangled in a spare blanket, stirring at the light with a grumble and one flattened ear. Mei was already up, moving quietly around the small room, pulling her uniform together, casting glances at Rin every few seconds like she was checking a pot that might boil over.

Rin sat up. Everything protested. She ignored it and swung her legs off the bed.

"Nope." Mei was across the room in an instant. "No, no. You fell out of a building yesterday. You are taking a day off. Two, maybe. I'll bring you food. I'll take notes. You are going to lie there and heal like a normal—" She caught herself on the word, because they both knew normal had left the conversation permanently. "Like a person."

A week ago Rin would have lied. Spun a flat excuse, deflected, gone anyway and let Mei think whatever she thought. But the door between them was open now, and Rin had decided she wasn't going to be the one to keep shutting it.

"I can't," she said. "Not today. I need to talk to my professors."

Mei blinked. "Your — professors?"

"Vomi." Rin said it and stopped there for a beat, because there was a second name and the second name was one she wasn't going to say out loud in this room — not the red-eyed reason, not yet, maybe not ever. "There was a lecture I blew off. About genetics. A theory I decided was a bedtime story and stopped listening to." She met Mei's eyes. "After yesterday I don't think it was a theory. I need to hear it again. Straight."

On the floor, Yuki propped herself up on one elbow, hair a disaster, suddenly awake. "The dormant gene thing," she said. "The heal-a-bruise-overnight thing. Yeah. Yeah, you need to hear that again." She nodded, serious under the bedhead. "Go. That one's worth getting out of bed for."

Mei chewed her lip, worry warring with the new understanding she was still learning to carry. "Okay," she said finally. "Okay. But you take it easy, and you tell me if it's too much." Then something occurred to her, and she brightened, crossing to her desk. "Oh — wait. Actually. Hang on."

She came back with a notebook and pressed it into Rin's hands.
