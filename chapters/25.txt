Chapter Twenty-Five — The Setup

Saturday evening, and Rin's phone had been quiet for days — until it wasn't.

The contract came in from a number she didn't know. That alone was odd; her work came through a small handful of brokers she'd learned to read, and this was none of them. But the packet was clean and professional: a name, a face, a set of locations pinned to the minute, all of it laid out neater than her usual brokers managed. The photo showed an ordinary man — soft, unremarkable, the kind of face you'd forget in a crowd. Nothing about him read threat.

And the fee at the bottom read ¥100,000.

Rin looked at that number twice. It was ten times what a mark like this should pay — more than she'd ever been offered for a single job. A soft man, exact coordinates, an obscene payday. Every instinct she had said a thing that generous had a hook in it somewhere.

But she needed the money. And, if she was honest with herself the way she'd been trying to be lately, she needed the action — it had been days, her hands were restless, and a body built for the edge did not enjoy sitting still.

Across the room, Mei looked up from her desk and gave her a look.

Rin held up the phone by way of answer.

Mei's face did its small worried thing, but she'd learned the rules now. She crossed the room and took Rin's hand and squeezed it once. "Be careful," she said. "I mean it. Every time you say it'll be easy, I get a little more suspicious of the word."

Rin almost laughed. "This one actually is. One soft target, no muscle in the photo. After the last few weeks?" She flexed her free hand, felt the new strength sitting under it. "This won't make me break a sweat."

She got ready in the dark the way she always did, and her mind ran while her hands worked.

She'd been jogging every morning since the hilltop. Every single day, pushing the pace past comfortable, chasing the burn — because Faith had gotten her speed from track, from running, from years of asking her body to go faster than it wanted to, and if the gene built in whatever direction you pushed it, then Rin could build speed the same way. She could already feel the difference. Her endurance had another floor under it now, and her legs answered quicker than they used to. Not Faith's frame-skipping blur — not yet, maybe not for a long time — but more. Enough that a job like tonight's wouldn't need any of it.

She caught Mei's eye one last time at the door. Mei mouthed it again — careful — and Rin nodded, and left.

Yuki was waiting outside, already dressed for the night, ears up and grinning.

"You don't have to come," Rin said. "This one's nothing. One guy, no crew. It'll be over before it's interesting — genuinely not worth your time."

"Uh-huh." Yuki fell into step beside her, entirely unbothered. "You said that, and then I'm coming anyway, because you can't get rid of me that easy, boss. We're a duo. Duos don't sit home because one job looks small." She bounced on her toes. "Besides. Your 'small jobs' have a real bad habit lately."

Rin sighed.

Yuki beamed, delighted, and that was that.

Across the city, Kyoya walked into a park and found it disappointing.

The devil woman had told him — that pleasant, poisonous voice in his ear again, she'll be there tonight, go and take what's yours — and he'd come, alone, because Jeanne still hadn't turned up at the compound and every other body he had was out grinding Amanda's endless quota. He didn't like being alone for this. But he'd have come with no one at all if it meant getting his hands on Rin Aizawa, and that was the part that gnawed at him even now, standing in the dark grass.

Because all he saw was an ordinary man loitering by a path, checking his phone, waiting for someone who wasn't coming.

Kyoya rolled the tension out of his neck and tried, for the hundredth time, to shake the feeling that he was being walked — led around by his own obsession like a dog on a leash he couldn't see the end of. He knew, in some buried and increasingly quiet part of himself, that this wasn't right. That he kept deciding to quit and then somehow un-deciding it. That a blonde woman kept appearing at exactly the moments his resolve wavered.

He knew it. And he could not, for the life of him, stop.

"One more dance," he muttered to no one, and grinned, and settled in to wait.

Rin and Yuki took the rooftops toward the park, quick and quiet, the city sliding by below.

"Anything?" Rin asked as they closed in. "Ears?"

Yuki slowed, tilting her head, ears swiveling. "One guy," she reported. "Just the one. Walking around, talking to himself, kind of… I can't make out the words. He sounds — I dunno. Off." She frowned. "But it's just him. No crew. No van. Nothing else moving down there."

Just him. It should have been reassuring. It wasn't.

They came up on the park's edge, and Rin crouched at the parapet and looked down at the soft man standing alone by the path, exactly where the packet said he'd be, exactly as harmless as his photo. And the bad feeling she'd had since the fee first flashed on her phone dug in deeper. She scanned the treeline, the paths, the shadows between the lamps. Nothing. No ambush she could find.

Which, she was learning, was its own kind of warning.

"Stay sharp," she murmured. "Something's off about this. The money was too—"

"On it, I'll grab him." Yuki was already dropping down off the roof, light and eager, springing across the grass toward the mark before Rin could finish. She closed the distance in a blink and took the man down clean, no fuss, pinning him easily.

"Okay, buddy," Yuki said, cheerful, "real quick — who sent you, what's the job, and why's somebody paying a fortune for a guy who folds like laundry?"

The man just gaped at her, terrified and utterly bewildered. "I — I don't — I don't know what you're talking about, I'm not — I'm just here for a date! A woman messaged me, blonde, she said meet here tonight, that's — that's all, I swear, I don't know anything—"

Yuki blinked down at him. "A date? Nobody sent you to—" She looked up, confusion all over her face. "Rin, this guy doesn't know anything, he thinks he's here to meet some blonde—"

The word blonde landed in Rin's chest like a stone dropping.

"Yuki—"

A green shadow detached from the dark.

It came fast — faster than the soft evening had any right to hold — and Yuki caught the motion at the last instant and twisted toward it, getting an arm up on pure reflex. The blade still found her. It sheared across her guard and into her side, and the deflection was the only reason it didn't cut deeper. Yuki cried out — a sound Rin had never heard her make — and the force of it threw her clear off the pinned man and across the grass, where she hit the ground and rolled and came to a stop, curled around her side, blood already dark between her fingers.

Wounded. Badly. Not a scratch, not a spar-graze — a real, serious, wrong-colored wound.

Rin was off the roof before she'd decided to move, hitting the grass in a run, everything in her narrowing to the shape of her friend bleeding in the dark.

Kyoya straightened up from where the strike had carried him, giant blade wet in his hand, and looked down at Yuki with an expression of genuine, pouting disappointment.

"Oh, she moved," he sighed, like a child whose toy had broken wrong. "That was supposed to be cleaner. Ah, well. She'll bleed out slow instead, I suppose, unless—" He turned, and his flat bright eyes found Rin sprinting toward him, and the disappointment melted off his face and became something radiant and terrible and thrilled.

"—unless you'd like to do something about it." The grin stretched wide. "There's my fire." He rolled the blade in his grip, planting himself between Rin and her wounded friend, savoring every second. "I've been dreaming about this. Just you and me this time — no cat to save you, no red woman, no windows to throw you out of. One more dance, Rin Aizawa. Let's settle it properly." His eyes glittered. "One way or another, I'm walking out of this park with you."

She had never wanted to win a fight so badly in her life. And she had never been less sure she could.
